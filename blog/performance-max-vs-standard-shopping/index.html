Performance Max vs Standard Shopping: Which Should UK E-Commerce Stores Use?

Performance Max vs Standard Shopping: real differences, data thresholds, and how we structure UK e-commerce accounts to get the most from both.

Performance Max vs Standard Shopping: which should I use?

For most established UK e-commerce accounts, Performance Max and Standard Shopping work best together rather than as a straight either/or choice. Performance Max suits accounts generating 30-50 conversions per month or more, with a clean feed and accurate conversion tracking. Standard Shopping gives more control, better search term visibility and more predictable behaviour, making it the stronger starting point for newer accounts or high-priority products where ROAS precision matters.

When Google retired Smart Shopping and moved everyone to Performance Max, it created a genuine question for store owners: is Standard Shopping still worth running, or has PMAX replaced it entirely? In the accounts we manage, the answer is nearly always that both are in play, structured deliberately to use the strengths of each rather than treating them as competitors.

What Performance Max Actually Does (and What It Gives Up)

Performance Max is a single campaign type that runs across all of Google's advertising inventory from one budget: Google Search, Google Shopping, Display Network, YouTube, Gmail and Google Maps. You supply a product feed, creative assets (images, headlines, videos, logos) and a conversion target. Google's machine learning then decides where, when and to whom to show ads, optimising towards your goal.

The appeal is real. One campaign, full Google coverage, Smart Bidding making real-time decisions based on signals your account cannot manually process. In the accounts we manage, PMAX at full strength surfaces products in places a Shopping-only campaign never reaches, capturing demand from YouTube and Display alongside high-intent Shopping queries.

The trade-off is control and transparency. You cannot view a full search term report. You cannot bid by device or audience segment in the traditional sense. Identifying which channel (Shopping vs YouTube vs Display) is generating your ROAS requires digging into asset group reports and campaign-level insights that give you far less granularity than classic campaign types. Google's own overview of Performance Max campaigns describes the full channel scope.

What Standard Shopping Still Does Well

Standard Shopping campaigns run exclusively on Google Shopping placements: the Shopping tab, Shopping units in search results and Google Images. You control bids at the product group level, set campaign priorities, and can access search term reports (limited since 2020, but still considerably more visibility than PMAX offers).

For accounts where data volume is modest, Standard Shopping with a target ROAS bidding strategy tends to behave more predictably. Smart Bidding inside a Standard Shopping campaign is drawing on the same machine learning infrastructure as PMAX, but within a narrower channel, which makes the optimisation task more manageable when conversion data is thin. The Standard Shopping campaign structure gives you product group granularity that PMAX's asset group model does not replicate.

Standard Shopping also remains the right tool for negative keyword precision, brand vs non-brand traffic splits, and protecting specific products where you have a clear ROAS target and do not want algorithm flexibility getting in the way.

The Conversion Data Threshold That Changes the Decision

Both campaign types rely on Smart Bidding, and Smart Bidding needs conversion volume to operate effectively. The broadly accepted floor is around 30-50 conversions per month at the campaign level. Below that threshold, the algorithm is learning from too few signals and will often make poor budget allocation decisions.

In a new account or a store launching its first Google Ads campaigns, PMAX can spend aggressively on lower-quality Display and YouTube inventory before it has enough data to understand what a real buyer looks like. A Standard Shopping campaign on a conservative target ROAS is typically a better starting point: it constrains the problem to Shopping inventory (where purchase intent is highest) and accumulates conversion data more efficiently before you widen the funnel.

Once an account is generating consistent volume (in our experience, 50 or more conversions per month across the account before leaning heavily into PMAX), the algorithm has enough signal to use the broader channel set productively. Our guide on Google Ads bidding strategy for e-commerce covers the Smart Bidding readiness criteria in more detail.

When to Use Performance Max

PMAX tends to deliver its best results when three things are in place: a clean, well-optimised product feed (see our Google Shopping feed optimisation guide); accurate conversion tracking that captures purchase value correctly; and at least 60-90 days of consistent conversion history in the account.

For stores with broad catalogues and solid data, PMAX's reach advantage is genuine. In the cycling and outdoor accounts we manage, PMAX captures seasonal demand through YouTube and Display that Standard Shopping simply cannot access. Pair it with strong creative assets and a well-segmented feed and it can operate across the full Google ecosystem without manual intervention.

Feed-only PMAX (adding no creative assets to the campaign) is a useful transitional step. Without assets, Google defaults to Shopping inventory only, so the campaign behaves similarly to Smart Shopping. This lets you test PMAX's bidding engine without immediately opening up the full multi-channel footprint, which is a sensible approach if you want the algorithm upgrade but are not ready to manage Display and YouTube placements alongside it.

When Standard Shopping Remains the Better Starting Point

Standard Shopping is still the right choice for new accounts, new product ranges without conversion history, and stores spending less than around £1,500-£2,500 per month on Shopping. Below that spend level there is typically not enough conversion volume to justify PMAX's broader mandate.

It is also the correct tool when you need search term transparency. If you are building out negative keyword lists, refining which products match which queries, or separating brand from generic traffic, Standard Shopping gives you the visibility to do that work properly. PMAX provides category-level search term insights but not the query-level data that drives serious negative keyword work.

A common pattern in underperforming accounts we audit is PMAX cannibalising brand search terms and taking credit for conversions that would have happened anyway through organic or direct traffic. Standard Shopping campaigns with brand-negative keyword lists can protect that efficiency. If your PMAX is underdelivering specifically, the post on Performance Max not working covers the most common causes and fixes.

The Hybrid Structure We Actually Use

Most of the accounts we manage run PMAX and Standard Shopping simultaneously, with deliberate structure rather than overlap. The typical set-up: a PMAX campaign for broad acquisition across the full catalogue, targeting new customer demand; and one or more Standard Shopping campaigns for high-margin or high-ROAS hero products where precision matters more than reach.

Account-level negative keywords apply across both campaign types. We monitor PMAX's channel insights monthly to catch budget drifting towards low-quality Display or YouTube placements. When PMAX ROAS dips, the first diagnostic is always the channel breakdown: Shopping-originated ROAS and Display-originated ROAS are often very different numbers blended into a single figure that can look misleading on the surface.

This structure requires active management. Asset group performance, search term category insights and budget allocation all need regular review. It is not complex, but it is not passive either. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Performance Max replacing Standard Shopping?

Google phased out Smart Shopping in favour of Performance Max, but Standard Shopping campaigns are still available and actively supported. Google has not announced a deadline for Standard Shopping removal. In practice, many well-structured accounts run both campaign types together.

Can I run Performance Max and Standard Shopping at the same time?

Yes. Running both in the same account is common practice. Performance Max typically takes priority in the auction, so you will need account-level negative keywords and campaign priority settings to control which campaign serves for which queries or products.

Why is my Performance Max ROAS lower than my old Shopping campaigns?

Performance Max reports blended ROAS across all channels (Search, Shopping, Display, YouTube, Gmail, Maps), so its figures are not directly comparable to a Shopping-only campaign. If PMAX is spending heavily on Display or YouTube, its ROAS will typically look lower even if Shopping-originated revenue is healthy. Check the asset group breakdown and channel insights inside the campaign.

What is feed-only Performance Max and when should I use it?

Feed-only Performance Max is a PMAX campaign with no creative assets (no images, headlines or videos) added. Without assets, Google can only serve Shopping inventory, so the campaign behaves similarly to a Smart Shopping campaign. It is useful for stores that want Performance Max's bidding engine without full multi-channel expansion.
