Google Merchant Center Shipping Mismatch UK

A Google Merchant Center shipping mismatch in the UK is a delivery price in the feed that is lower than checkout. Products get disapproved, and Shopping volume drops.

JBy Jack Goldsmith · Published 22 November 2026 · 6 min read

A Google Merchant Center shipping mismatch in the UK means the delivery price in Merchant Center, or in the product feed, is lower than the price on your checkout. Google calls that inaccurate delivery costs. It can warn the account, disapprove the products, or stop Shopping and Performance Max until the two prices match. The usual trigger is free delivery: the feed says £0, or free on every order, while checkout still charges under a basket threshold.

By Jack Goldsmith, Founder & Performance Marketer, Social Surge · 22 November 2026

In the accounts we manage, spend falls before anyone opens Merchant Center. Products that were serving last week are blocked because a courier price and a feed price disagree. Fix the rate before you touch target ROAS.

Why a shipping mismatch disapproves your products

Google's page on inaccurate delivery costs states the rule. Rates in Merchant Center, or in the product data, are lower than the rates shown during checkout. A higher rate in Merchant Center is allowed. If you cannot match checkout exactly, Google says to slightly overestimate.

Set the account policy first, and override it only where a group of SKUs really differs. A shipping price in the feed overrides that policy. One bestseller at 0 GBP for the United Kingdom undoes the setup.

An automated Shopify update can overwrite a manual fix, and a third-party delivery app is not imported. Google's delivery settings guide says you enter that policy yourself. With more than one UK option, Google shows the lowest cost, so a service wrongly set to free is the price in the ad.

Products disapproved for inaccurate shipping costs

Read the warning email first and look for one shared cause. Rewriting titles will not clear a delivery disapproval. A feed optimisation pass is a different job. These are the patterns behind products disapproved for inaccurate shipping costs:

  • The feed says free. Checkout does not. The row is 0 GBP because the homepage says free UK delivery. Checkout still charges £4.95 under £60. Google compares checkout, not the banner.
  • The thresholds disagree. Merchant Center says free over £50. The site says free over £75. Orders under the real line fail the check.
  • A code unlocks the free rate. The crawl does not type the code, so checkout shows the paid price.
  • The product row overrides the account. The policy is right, then the feed sends 0 GBP, and that price wins.
  • An accessory rate sits on a bulky SKU. £3.95 on a bike, a tent or a tackle box, then a surcharge at checkout. UK delivery can be set by postcode, including a Highlands rate.

UK carrier rates, which Google illustrates with UPS, TNT and Hermes, need weight and dimensions. If that table will not fit, set Merchant Center a little higher than checkout, never lower.

Free delivery threshold not in Merchant Center

If the free delivery threshold is not in Merchant Center, Google applies the rate you did submit to every order. Free, with no condition, means free on a £12 item. A paid rate, with no threshold, stays paid even when that product already costs more than your free-delivery line.

Go to Delivery and returns, under Products and shop, open Delivery policies, and add a policy. You can keep up to 20 per country. For the United Kingdom, in GBP, choose Free delivery over a specific order value. Google's example is free delivery for orders over £100. Enter your threshold, and the fee charged below it.

Do not choose No delivery below a specific order value if you still ship and only charge a fee. That option means you refuse the order. Checkout does not.

The product price, not the basket

If the feed already sends shipping, add free_shipping_threshold. The format is the country, then the amount, with a full stop as the decimal: GB:75.00 GBP. Currency must match the price. Do not send two United Kingdom thresholds. With the service sub-attribute, send one service level, not standard and expedited together.

Google checks that threshold against the product's own price, not the basket. A £29 light with free delivery over £75 should show the paid rate, because one unit at checkout is charged. Setting it to 0 GBP is what gets it disapproved. A £2,400 bike already over £75 can show free. Those products need two policies, not one rate.

Bulky cycling, fishing and outdoor orders

One policy breaks a mixed catalogue. In cycling, lights can share a flat rate and frames often cannot. Outdoor splits a head torch from a tent, and fishing splits terminal tackle from a seatbox. Label each group, attach a policy, and send weight on the heavy lines. A missing weight does not make delivery free. Do not exclude those SKUs to hide the warning unless they lose money after postage. If the rate is right and the product still loses money, use custom labels by margin.

When not to appeal, or change the bid

Appeal only after a test order matches the ad: one product under the threshold, one over it, on a mainland postcode and on any postcode you surcharge, with no code typed in. Merchant Center must meet that checkout price or sit above it.

Once the fix is live, Google says an appeal starts a crawl that usually finishes within 24-48 hours. There can be a limit on reviews. Do not spend one while checkout still charges more than the feed.

This is not a bidding problem. Lost products pull spend and conversion value down together, so leave target ROAS alone for a week after they return. A wider block, including misrepresentation, starts with the suspension guide. Approved products on the wrong page are covered in why Performance Max is not working.

Our published average is 18.3x ROAS, with floors of 13x and above in cycling, 12x and above in fishing and 14x and above in outdoor, and a best month of 35.3x. That is not a forecast for products Google has pulled. Judge what is approved, and profit after postage. Do not fund disapproved SKUs into late November: the Black Friday budget assumes they can show. Google Ads management is £1,500 a month. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Google Merchant Center shipping mismatch in the UK?

A Google Merchant Center shipping mismatch in the UK means the delivery price in Merchant Center, or in the product feed, is lower than checkout. Google treats that as inaccurate delivery costs, so products or the account can be disapproved. If you cannot match checkout exactly, slightly overestimate in Merchant Center. Never set the feed cheaper than the shopper pays.

Why are products disapproved for inaccurate shipping costs?

Products are disapproved for inaccurate shipping costs when Merchant Center is cheaper than the website. Typical UK causes are a feed at 0 GBP, a free-over threshold that does not match checkout, a product shipping price overriding the account, or a Shopify app that never synced. The delivery currency has to be GBP on a UK offer.

How do I add a free delivery threshold in Merchant Center?

Open Delivery and returns under Products and shop, then Delivery policies, and add a UK policy in GBP. Choose Free delivery over a specific order value, with the same threshold as checkout and the paid rate below it. Do not choose No delivery below a specific order value if you still ship and only charge a fee. If the feed sets shipping, add free_shipping_threshold, for example GB:75.00 GBP. Google checks that threshold against the product price, not the basket.

Will fixing the shipping mismatch bring Shopping traffic back?

Impressions come back only after Google recrawls, usually within 24-48 hours of a review. Do not raise target ROAS or cut the budget in that same week. If checkout still charges more than the feed, the review fails, and Google says there can be a limit on how many reviews you can request.
