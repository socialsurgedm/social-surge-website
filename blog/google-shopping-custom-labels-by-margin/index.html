Google Shopping Custom Labels by Margin: Stop Scaling Unprofitable SKUs

Shopify Google Ads conversions stopped tracking: the August 2026 additional scripts cutoff, how to confirm your tag died, and three paths to restore purchase data without double-counting.

JBy Jack Goldsmith · Published 15 November 2026 · 6 min read

ContentsWhat Changed on 26 August 2026How to Confirm Your Tag DiedHow to Fix It: Three PathsWhat Not to Do While the Tag Is BrokenHow Long Does Smart Bidding Take to Recover?Frequently Asked Questions

Google Shopping custom labels by margin are how you stop Performance Max and standard Shopping from scaling the SKUs that look fine on ROAS and still lose money. ROAS is revenue divided by ad spend. It does not know your VAT, your postage, your returns, or the fact that a bestseller has a 20% gross margin. A custom label puts that fact somewhere Google Ads can bid against.

By Jack Goldsmith, Founder & Performance Marketer, Social Surge · 15 November 2026

In the accounts we manage, the usual pattern is a handful of hero products with real margin, and a long tail that wins clicks because the price is low. Smart Bidding treats a conversion as a conversion. If you tell it to hit a target ROAS of 4, it will happily buy revenue on a product where £4 back on £1 of ads does not cover the goods.

Why ROAS hides the products that lose money

Take a £40 product with a 25% gross margin. The goods and inbound cost leave £10. VAT on the selling price, payment fees and the postage you actually pay can cut that again. If Google Ads spends £8 to sell one, the campaign can show a 5x ROAS and the order still contributes almost nothing, or a loss once the return lands. Scale that SKU because "ROAS is above target" and you buy yourself a busier warehouse.

Performance Max makes this worse when every product sits in one asset group. The campaign reports one ROAS. The products inside it do not share one margin. Excluding a low-margin SKU can make account ROAS rise while revenue dips. Owners who only watch revenue put the product back and call the test a failure.

This is also why a single target ROAS for the whole catalogue is a blunt tool. The target that is right for a 60% margin accessory will starve it, because the campaign is spending its learning budget on cheap, low-margin clicks that convert more easily.

A simple margin label scheme

You have five custom labels, custom_label_0 to custom_label_4. Use one of them for margin and leave the others for season, price band or returns. Google documents the field as a campaign-organisation tool, not something customers see, in Merchant Center custom labels.

A scheme we can actually run without a data team:

protect: gross margin about 50% or more, and not a returns magnet. These can take more budget.

core: about 30-50%. These stay in the main campaign at the normal target.

cap: under about 30% after the shipping you pay, or a returns rate that wipes the margin. These are excluded from scale campaigns, or they sit in a campaign with a much harder target and a hard budget cap.

Calculate the band from landed cost, not from the RRP discount you show on site. A product "reduced from £90 to £45" is not a high-margin product if it costs you £38 landed. Refresh the label when costs move. A label set in January and forgotten by October will scale the wrong things into peak season.

If you do not trust the cost prices, do not invent bands to look precise. Start with the obvious losers: free-delivery SKUs under £20, and anything the warehouse already knows comes back. A rough exclude list beats a perfect label you will not maintain.

How to use the labels in Shopping and Performance Max

In standard Shopping, subdivide the listing group by the margin label. Put most of the budget on protect and core. Set the cap group to a low bid or exclude it. One shared budget across all three bands recreates the original problem.

In Performance Max, the label does nothing until an asset group or listing group filter uses it. Split "all products" into protect/core and everything else, or exclude the cap label from the only asset group. Check the product report a week later. If the excluded SKUs are still spending, the filter is not applied to the asset group that has the budget. That is the usual miss, not a Merchant Center delay.

Give the change two to three weeks before you judge it, and do not move target ROAS in the same week. Otherwise you cannot tell whether profit moved because of the products or because Smart Bidding was reset. Keep a note of revenue, ad spend and, if you have it, gross profit after ads. ROAS alone will flatter the test.

What to do with the low-margin products

Excluding a product from Shopping is not the same as deleting it from the business. Some low-margin SKUs are there to win the first order, and the profit shows up in the repeat purchase or the accessory attached at checkout. If email or subscriptions carry that, keep a small capped campaign so you can still see the product. Do not let Performance Max spend the Christmas budget on it just because it converts.

The better commercial fix is often on the site, not in Ads. Raise the price, raise the free-delivery threshold, or stop advertising the variant that only exists to match a competitor's headline price. Ads cannot invent margin that the product does not have. Product exclusions are the emergency brake. Price and shipping are the repair.

Feed quality still matters. A margin label on a product with a wrong GTIN, a missing size, or a shipping price that does not match the site will not get a clean test. Fix disapprovals before you read anything into the new campaign's ROAS.

When custom labels are the wrong fix

Do not build margin labels to hide a broken conversion tag. If purchases are under-counted, every band looks worse and you will exclude products that are actually fine. Fix tracking first.

Do not split into five bands on a catalogue that gets thirty orders a month. Smart Bidding needs volume inside each group. Three bands, or even a single exclude list, is the right size until the protect group can produce a steady flow of conversions on its own.

And do not expect the label to correct a Performance Max campaign that is mostly spending on your own brand name. That ROAS is a different illusion. Brand separation and margin labels solve different problems. If you want both checked against the live feed, that is what we do in Google Ads management and in a free audit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a custom label in Google Shopping?

A custom label is a field you add in Merchant Center, custom_label_0 to custom_label_4, that does not show on the product page. You use it to group products for Shopping listing groups and Performance Max, for example by margin band, season or returns rate.

How many margin bands should I use?

Three is enough to start: protect (roughly 50% gross margin and above), core (about 30-50%), and exclude or cap (under about 30% after the shipping you actually pay). Add a fourth only when a band is large enough to have its own conversion history.

Will excluding low-margin products lower my ROAS?

Often the opposite. Those SKUs were spending at an acceptable ROAS and still losing cash. Taking them out can drop revenue and raise the ROAS you have left. Judge the change on gross profit after ads, not on last month's revenue chart.

Can I use custom labels if I only run Performance Max?

Yes. Asset groups and listing group filters can use custom labels. If you only have one asset group pointed at all products, the label does nothing until you split the group or exclude the weak label.
