How Much Does a Google Ads Agency Cost for UK E-Commerce?

Pricing tiers, the flat-fee vs percentage-of-spend question, what a retainer should actually include, and the red flags that signal a bad deal before you sign.

By Jack Goldsmith, Founder & Performance Marketer, Social Surge · 29 October 2026

How much does a Google Ads agency cost for UK e-commerce?

UK Google Ads agencies typically charge £500-£3,000+ per month for e-commerce accounts, depending on account size and management depth. Specialist e-commerce PPC agencies with flat-fee pricing generally sit in the £1,000-£2,000/month range. Percentage-of-spend models add 10-20% on top of your media budget and become expensive quickly as spend scales. A flat monthly fee aligns the agency's incentive with performance, not with burning budget.

How much does a Google Ads agency cost for UK e-commerce is one of the first questions every store owner asks, and it is genuinely hard to answer without knowing what you are comparing. The market runs from freelancers charging £300-£500/month with multiple clients and minimal active management to large agencies quoting 15% of spend on budgets of tens of thousands of pounds. This guide cuts through that range, explains what each tier delivers, and gives you the tools to decide whether a given fee is fair.

What UK Google Ads Agencies Actually Charge for E-Commerce

Pricing in the UK PPC market clusters into four rough tiers. These are broad ranges based on what we observe in the market; individual agencies vary considerably within each band.

Tier 1: Budget-managed (£300-£700/month)

Typically a freelancer or micro-agency managing 10-15+ clients simultaneously. Deliverables usually mean campaign setup plus occasional check-ins. There is rarely a dedicated account strategist, and monthly changes may amount to a handful of bid adjustments. For a store turning over less than £250k/year with a modest ad budget, this can be a reasonable starting point. For anyone spending more than £3,000-£4,000/month on Google Ads, the fee-to-spend ratio looks acceptable, but the management intensity rarely matches what a live e-commerce account needs.

Tier 2: Mid-market (£800-£1,500/month)

Where most regional UK agencies and digital marketing generalists sit. You get a named account manager, regular reporting and reactive optimisation. The risk at this tier is the generalist problem: the account manager splits their week between Google Ads, Meta Ads, SEO and sometimes email, meaning no single channel gets deep specialism. E-commerce accounts running Performance Max alongside Standard Shopping need someone who works inside the platform daily, not an hour or two per week.

Tier 3: Specialist e-commerce PPC (£1,200-£2,500/month)

Dedicated PPC agencies and consultants who work exclusively or primarily with e-commerce clients. In the accounts we manage, this tier is where the real work happens: feed audits and ongoing Merchant Center monitoring, Smart Bidding strategy matched to conversion volume, search term reviews, audience signal testing and conversion tracking verification. The fee is higher, but the management depth is categorically different. This is the range most mid-sized UK e-commerce stores should be comparing when they go to market for a new agency.

Tier 4: Enterprise or full-service (£2,500-£10,000+/month)

Large agencies with dedicated strategists, creative teams and technology stacks. Justified for brands spending £50,000+/month across channels, running complex international campaigns, or needing integrations across paid search, paid social and programmatic. Most e-commerce stores in the £1m-£5m revenue range do not need this tier and will not get meaningfully better results from it.

Flat Fee vs Percentage of Ad Spend: Which Pricing Model Is Better for E-Commerce?

This is the most important structural question when comparing agencies, and the answer matters more than the headline number.

How percentage-of-spend pricing works

An agency charges 10-20% of whatever you spend on media each month. At £5,000/month ad spend and a 15% fee, that is £750 in management costs. At £20,000/month spend, it becomes £3,000 for managing the same number of campaigns. The work does not scale proportionally with spend; the fee does.

The structural problem is the incentive. An agency on percentage pricing earns more money every time you increase your budget, whether or not that increase improves your ROAS. In the accounts we manage, we have seen stores pushed to scale spend before campaigns had the conversion data to support it, precisely because a bigger budget meant a bigger management fee. That is not a criticism of every percentage-fee agency, but the conflict of interest is real and structural.

How flat-fee pricing works

A fixed monthly retainer regardless of your media budget. The agency earns the same whether you spend £3,000 or £30,000/month. The incentive shifts entirely to performance: the only way the agency grows its revenue from your account is if you get results and stay, or expand into additional services. That is a much healthier alignment.

Flat fees can feel expensive at low spend levels. If you are spending £1,000/month on ads and paying £1,500/month management, the blended cost is significant. Most specialist e-commerce agencies have a minimum ad spend requirement for this reason, typically £3,000-£5,000/month on media, because below that threshold there is not enough data for Smart Bidding or Performance Max to function properly. Our view on minimum budgets for e-commerce is covered in the Google Ads budget guide.

What a Google Ads Retainer Should Actually Include

The deliverable gap between agencies at the same price point is enormous. Before signing, get specific answers on what is included. Here is what a competent e-commerce Google Ads retainer should cover:

  • Conversion tracking audit and verification. Not just "tracking is set up" but a check that the right events are firing, values are passing correctly, and that enhanced conversions are active. Broken tracking is the single most common cause of poor Smart Bidding performance. See our conversion tracking guide for e-commerce for exactly what to check.
  • Google Merchant Center and feed monitoring. For Shopping and Performance Max campaigns, feed health determines which products show and at what cost. A retainer without feed monitoring is incomplete for any e-commerce store.
  • Campaign structure and bidding strategy, documented. Which campaigns run, which bidding strategies apply, and why. An agency should be able to explain in plain English whether you are on Performance Max, Standard Shopping or a hybrid, and what triggers a change. Our Performance Max vs Standard Shopping comparison gives useful context here.
  • Search term reviews and negative keyword management. Budget wasted on irrelevant queries is quiet and cumulative. Monthly or fortnightly search term audits with documented negatives are non-negotiable. More detail in our negative keywords guide.
  • Weekly reporting with revenue and ROAS. Not impressions. Not clicks. Revenue, ROAS, CPA and conversion value, with platform-level access so you can verify every number yourself.
  • A named contact and defined communication cadence. Who do you email when something breaks? How quickly do they respond? Get this in writing before you sign.

Google's own guidance on working with a Google Ads professional is worth reading before any agency sales call. It sets out minimum expectations for qualified practitioners.

Google Ads Agency Red Flags for E-Commerce Stores

These are the signals that a retainer is likely to underdeliver, regardless of what the proposal says. Our marketing agency red flags guide covers the broader version; below are the Google Ads-specific ones to watch for when hiring for e-commerce.

They run your campaigns inside their own ad accounts

If an agency will not give you admin access to your own Google Ads account, walk away. Your conversion history, audience data, bidding models and years of account learning live in that account. If you ever leave, you lose all of it. No legitimate agency asks for this trade-off. You should own your accounts from day one, and the agency should work inside them as a managed access partner.

They report clicks and impressions as primary metrics

A monthly report that leads with reach, click-through rate or engagement and buries revenue on page four is not written for you. It is written to look impressive while hiding the actual commercial results. Revenue and ROAS should open every report. If your agency cannot tell you what ROAS your campaigns delivered last month without a follow-up conversation, something is wrong.

They cannot show meaningful account change history

Google Ads logs every change made to an account. An agency doing real work has a detailed change history: negative keywords added, bid strategy adjustments, ad copy tested, audience signals refined, budget pauses during low-conversion periods. An account with two or three changes per month is not being actively managed. Ask for a screenshot of the change history from the past 30 days before you sign anything.

Long minimum contract terms

PPC needs 60-90 days to show meaningful results, and a good agency will tell you that honestly. But that is an argument for patience, not for a 12-month lock-in. Rolling monthly agreements keep the agency honest: the only way to retain your business is to perform. Long contracts serve the agency, not the advertiser.

Vague deliverables in the contract

"Ongoing optimisation" commits an agency to nothing. Before signing, ask: what specifically will you do in month one? What is your onboarding process? What is your response process when ROAS drops by 30%? Agencies that cannot answer these questions with specifics have not built a repeatable process. That matters enormously for e-commerce accounts where seasonality, feed changes and Smart Bidding fluctuations demand fast, systematic responses.

How to Work Out Whether a Management Fee Is Worth Paying

The calculation is straightforward. If a £1,500/month management fee on a £5,000/month ad budget generates a 6x ROAS (£30,000 in attributed revenue) that a self-managed account generating 2x ROAS (£10,000) would not, the fee pays for itself many times over. The relevant comparison is not management cost versus zero management cost. It is managed performance versus unmanaged performance, net of the fee.

In the accounts we manage, the average ROAS across our e-commerce client base is 18.3x. That number does not appear overnight: it takes the right campaign structure, bidding strategy, feed quality and conversion tracking working together, built and maintained by someone who works inside Google Ads every day. Most stores starting Google Ads without specialist support typically achieve 2-4x initially, which is often below the threshold needed to justify continued spend at all. Our ROAS benchmarks guide for UK e-commerce puts those numbers in context.

The fee is not the only cost of the agency relationship either. Time spent on briefings, reporting calls and chasing answers has real value. A good agency minimises that overhead through clear communication and proactive reporting. A poor one creates it through vague deliverables and reactive problem-solving. Factor both into your comparison.
