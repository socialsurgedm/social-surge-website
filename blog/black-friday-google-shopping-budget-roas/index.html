Black Friday Google Shopping Budget: How to Scale Spend Without Killing Your ROAS

Increasing budget for Black Friday is the easy part. Keeping ROAS alive while you do it is where most stores come unstuck. Here is how to scale spend step by step, adjust your tROAS for sale-price margins and use Performance Max seasonality adjustments without blowing up your account.

By Jack Goldsmith, Founder & Performance Marketer, Social Surge · 3 November 2026

How do you increase your Black Friday Google Shopping budget without killing ROAS?

Increase budget in steps of 15-20% every 2-3 days rather than doubling overnight, recalculate your tROAS target to reflect sale-price margins (not your year-round full-price benchmark), and apply a Performance Max seasonality adjustment only if your sale is a short 1-3 day event with a significant expected conversion rate spike. The margin maths is the step most stores skip: if you are discounting 25%, your break-even ROAS falls, and your unchanged tROAS target will throttle spend at exactly the moment search intent peaks.

Every November, store owners face the same tension: Black Friday is coming, demand is real, and doubling the budget feels both necessary and dangerous. That nervousness is well-founded. In the accounts we manage, Black Friday budget increases applied without adjusting tROAS targets or accounting for sale-price margins frequently deliver lower overall revenue than a steadier, better-structured approach. This guide covers the mechanics so you can plan with numbers, not instinct.

Why Black Friday Budgets Kill ROAS (It Is Not Just Auction Inflation)

The standard explanation is that CPCs spike at Black Friday because every advertiser piles into the same auctions at once. That is true: shopping auction competition typically rises sharply in the final week of November. But auction inflation is only part of the story, and often not the most costly part.

The bigger issue is that your tROAS target was calibrated on full-price margins. If your normal gross margin is 40% and you run a 20% discount event, your margin on Black Friday sales might fall to 20-25%. The ROAS you need to break even has dropped, but Smart Bidding is still trying to hit a threshold built on different product economics. It will not necessarily overspend. More likely it will throttle impressions in an attempt to hit a target that no longer reflects reality, and you will miss volume precisely when intent is at its seasonal peak.

For Performance Max specifically, there is a third factor. Google's guidance on budget changes notes that significant increases can affect campaign learning. For PMax, which runs multiple asset groups and channel types simultaneously, that instability matters more in a compressed sale window than it would in a standard Shopping campaign.

Recalculate Your tROAS for Sale-Price Margins Before Touching the Budget

Before increasing spend by a single pound, work out your break-even ROAS at your sale price. The formula is simple:

Break-even ROAS = 1 divided by your gross margin as a decimal

At a 40% gross margin, break-even ROAS is 2.5x. At a 25% margin (after a 20% price discount on a typical product), break-even ROAS is 4.0x. If your year-round tROAS target sits at 8x or 12x, built to deliver a comfortable profit at full price, Smart Bidding will correctly judge that most Black Friday auction opportunities cannot clear that bar. It will pull back. You will see impressions fall and revenue stall at the exact moment you were hoping to capitalise on demand.

In our experience, setting tROAS at 1.3-1.5x your sale-period break-even ROAS gives Smart Bidding enough room to compete aggressively while still protecting margin. If your sale-period break-even is 4x, a tROAS target of around 5-6x is a reasonable starting range. The exact multiplier depends on your category, your competitors' discount depth and your average order value, so treat these as starting points rather than fixed rules.

If you sell cycling or outdoor products and your Black Friday sale covers accessories at different margin rates to your full bikes or premium hardware, you will need to run this calculation per product group. That is where custom labels become essential, covered below.

How to Scale Budget Without Triggering a Learning Reset

The practical rule is to change budget by no more than 15-20% at a time, then wait 2-3 days before making the next increase. That incremental pace lets Smart Bidding adapt without forcing a full learning reset. For a campaign currently spending £200 per day, reaching a Black Friday peak of £500 per day is achievable through four or five sequential steps, starting in mid-November, with each step bedded in before the next.

For stores that have left it late (fewer than two weeks to Black Friday), there are two options. You can accept a faster ramp and some learning instability during the first 12-24 hours of your sale, or you can use a campaign-level automated budget rule to schedule the increases in advance. We prefer the latter: setting a time-based rule to lift budget on the morning of the 28th and reduce it on the 1st of December removes the temptation to panic-increase mid-sale when ROAS looks shaky for the first few hours, which it almost always does during a surge event.

Performance Max Seasonality Adjustments: What They Do and When to Use Them

A seasonality adjustment lets you signal to Smart Bidding that a short-term spike in conversion rate is expected. It is designed for sale events lasting 1-3 days where you anticipate a significant, abrupt change in conversion rate, not a rolling two-week promotional period.

When you apply one, Smart Bidding adjusts its conversion rate predictions upward for the specified window, which prompts more aggressive bidding to capture the expected demand. When the window closes, predictions return to baseline. Google's guidance on seasonality adjustments recommends using them only when you expect conversion rate to change by a meaningful amount, typically more than 30-50% relative to your baseline, and only for the duration of that change.

When a seasonality adjustment is appropriate: a 24-48 hour flash sale or a Cyber Monday promotion where conversion rate will spike sharply. When it is not appropriate: a two-week build-up to Black Friday, a modest 10-15% discount, or any scenario where the conversion rate change will be gradual rather than sudden. Overusing seasonality adjustments can push Smart Bidding's models in the wrong direction and take several days to settle afterward.

One thing seasonality adjustments do not control: your budget. They change how Smart Bidding bids in response to expected conversion rate changes. Budget and tROAS targets still need to be set correctly alongside them.

Cycling and Outdoor Stores: Black Friday Is Accessories, Not Bikes

In the accounts we manage for cycling brands and outdoor retailers, the November conversion pattern is consistent: gift-suitable accessories (lights, bike tools, gloves, waterproof layers, winter kit) see sharper conversion rate lifts than full bikes or big-ticket equipment. The reason is straightforward: Black Friday is when cyclists buy gifts for other cyclists, not when they invest in a new road bike for themselves.

That pattern has a practical consequence for budget allocation. If you lift your entire campaign budget uniformly, you are spending more on your lowest-converting November products (full bikes, premium hardware) alongside your highest-converting ones (accessories and gift-ready items). Using custom labels in your product feed to separate high-margin gift SKUs from lower-margin big-ticket inventory lets you apply different tROAS targets and budgets by product group. For a typical cycling account, we run accessories at a lower tROAS floor during Black Friday and keep the big-ticket segment on a tighter tROAS to protect margin on heavier price reductions.

If your feed does not yet use custom labels, mid-November is the practical deadline: feed changes propagate through Merchant Center within 24-48 hours, so you need them live before the main traffic surge begins.

Three Situations Where You Should Not Increase Your Black Friday Budget

Not every account is ready for scaling. In these three situations, increasing budget is likely to make performance worse rather than better:

  • Your Merchant Center has active product disapprovals. Scaling budget while products are disapproved means more spend competing for a narrower product set, which typically inflates CPC and compresses ROAS. Resolve disapprovals before increasing spend. Our guide on Merchant Center misrepresentation suspensions covers the most common feed errors that cause disapprovals.
  • Your ROAS is already below target in October. A Black Friday budget increase will typically amplify an existing underperformance problem rather than fix it. Diagnose the root cause first: tROAS set too high, feed quality issues, or a bidding strategy mismatch. Spending more into a broken structure just burns cash faster.
  • Your conversion tracking has not been verified since summer. Changes to Shopify's checkout in August 2026 removed additional scripts from the thank-you page, which broke purchase tags for many UK stores that had not migrated to the Shopify Google Ads app or server-side tracking. If you have not confirmed that your purchase conversion action is firing correctly, Smart Bidding may be optimising on incomplete data. Spending more into that situation is expensive and corrects nothing. If you are uncertain, book a free PPC audit while there is still time to fix the tracking before Black Friday.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I increase my Google Shopping budget for Black Friday?

In our experience, increase in steps of 15-20% every 2-3 days starting one to two weeks before the event, rather than doubling overnight. A sudden large increase can reset Performance Max's learning period and cause erratic bidding precisely when you need stability.

Should I lower my tROAS target during Black Friday?

Yes, if you are running a sale. Your year-round tROAS target is built on full-price margins. If you discount 20-30%, your break-even ROAS drops too, which means your usual tROAS target will throttle the campaign and you will miss volume at the exact moment intent peaks. Recalculate break-even ROAS on your sale-price margins and set tROAS just above that floor.

What is a Black Friday seasonality adjustment in Performance Max?

A seasonality adjustment is a signal you add to Performance Max in Google Ads to tell Smart Bidding that a short-term spike in conversion rate is expected. Google recommends using it for events lasting 1-3 days where conversion rates will change significantly. It is not a budget tool: it adjusts bids in response to expected conversion rate changes, not your spend cap.

Do cycling or outdoor stores need a different Black Friday Google Ads approach?

Yes. In the accounts we manage for cycling and outdoor brands, gift-suitable accessories such as lights, gloves, tools and winter kit see the sharpest conversion rate lifts in November, not full bikes or big-ticket items. Prioritising those SKUs through custom labels and separate campaigns, rather than lifting all budgets equally, tends to deliver better overall ROAS during the Black Friday window.
