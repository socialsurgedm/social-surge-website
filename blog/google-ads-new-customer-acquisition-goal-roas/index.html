Google Ads New Customer Acquisition Goal: Why Your ROAS Is Higher Than Your Revenue

The Google Ads new customer acquisition goal inflates your reported ROAS by adding a bonus value to every conversion attributed to a new buyer. If you have switched on "new customer value" bidding in Performance Max or Shopping, the ROAS figure on your dashboard includes that uplift. It is not the same as cash return on ad spend.

The Google Ads new customer acquisition goal inflates your reported ROAS by adding a bonus value to every conversion attributed to a new buyer. The adjusted figure is what Smart Bidding optimises against, and what the Conversion value column shows. Your store's actual revenue has not changed. The gap between the two widens every time the goal is set with a generous multiplier, or when a large share of your buyers are classified as new customers.

What the new customer goal does to your conversion value

When you enable the new customer acquisition goal (available in Performance Max, Shopping, and Search campaigns), you tell Google that a first purchase is worth more than a repeat one. That extra value is added to the Conversion value column immediately. If a new buyer spends £150 on a cycling accessory and your multiplier is 1.5x, Google logs £225 as the conversion value and bids toward a 1.5x inflated version of your ROAS target.

This is working as intended. The logic is that a customer who goes on to buy three or four times over the next two years is genuinely worth more than the first order implies. The problem is that platform ROAS includes the bonus, store revenue does not, and many store owners notice the gap only when they compare Google Ads against Shopify or WooCommerce at month end.

This adjustment is separate from conversion value rules, which modify values by geography or device. The new customer goal uplift sits on top and applies specifically to buyers the system classifies as first-time purchasers. Both adjustments can be active at the same time, compounding the gap between platform ROAS and cash return.

How Performance Max bids higher for new customers

Google identifies new customers using three signals: your Customer Match list (existing buyers whose emails you have uploaded), the new_customer parameter if you pass it via your conversion tag, and auto-detection based on purchase history within the last 540 days.

Where the system is confident a buyer is new, the campaign bids more aggressively. That pushes Performance Max toward more competitive inventory, which typically costs more per click. In the accounts we manage, campaigns with this goal active often show higher average CPCs than equivalent campaigns without it, because the system is deliberately paying a premium to reach first-time buyers. That is the trade you are making: accepting a higher cost per new order in exchange for a richer future customer base.

A thin or stale Customer Match list makes this worse. If Google cannot identify many existing customers, it over-classifies buyers as new, inflates the adjustment beyond its intended scope, and bids more than your business can justify. Google's guidance on new customer acquisition goals recommends uploading a Customer Match list covering at least 1,000 matched customers before activating this feature.

The new customer value multiplier: how big is the inflation?

Google offers two ways to set the uplift. A flat bonus adds a fixed pound amount to every new-customer conversion. The percentage impact varies by basket size: a flat £30 bonus adds 60% to a £50 order and only 3% to a £1,000 order.

A multiplier (rolling out from late October 2026 for UK advertisers) scales the adjustment with basket size. At 1.2x, a £100 order is treated as £120 in the Conversion value column. At 1.5x, it becomes £150. At 2x, it becomes £200.

The inflation to your reported ROAS depends on two things: the multiplier you set, and the share of your conversion value that comes from new customers. As a rough guide:

  • 1.2x multiplier, 40% of value from new customers: reported ROAS runs approximately 8% above cash ROAS.
  • 1.5x multiplier, 40% of value from new customers: approximately 20% above cash ROAS.
  • 2.0x multiplier, 40% of value from new customers: approximately 40% above cash ROAS.

Google's own starting guidance for the value setting is on the order of 2x a regular customer's order value. Many store owners set it there without working through what that does to the ROAS number they then act on. A campaign showing a 6x ROAS with a 2x multiplier and 40% new-customer share may be generating closer to 4.3x on actual revenue.

Why your tROAS target no longer means what it did

If you set a tROAS of 6x and then switch on new customer value bidding with a 1.5x multiplier and 40% of value from new buyers, Smart Bidding is now targeting a 6x return on the adjusted conversion value, not on order revenue. For a campaign with that profile, the real cash ROAS the system accepts is closer to 5x.

In the accounts we manage, we typically raise the tROAS target by roughly the same factor as the inflation when switching this goal on. If the multiplier adds 20% to reported value, we raise the target from 6x to around 7x to hold the same real efficiency threshold. Without that adjustment, the system quietly accepts a lower real return on every new-customer order, every day.

This matters most before a peak period. If you switch on the new customer goal in October without updating your tROAS, margins can be squeezed across Black Friday and Christmas because the system is spending to a target that no longer reflects the cash ROAS you need. How to find your real cash ROAS in-platform

Add two columns to your Google Ads reporting view:

  • Original conversion value: the conversion value before any lifecycle goal or value rule adjustments. This is the number closest to actual revenue.
  • New customer lifetime value: the adjustment itself, the uplift portion only. Subtracting this from Conversion value gives you the real order total.

Cross-check that figure against your Shopify or WooCommerce order revenue for the same date range. The gap should be small and explained by attribution differences, not by systematic uplift. If Conversion value consistently runs 20-30% above store revenue and you have the new customer goal active, the adjustment is almost certainly the source.

Do not report Conversion value divided by cost to your finance team, or use it to decide whether to scale, while this goal is active. See Google's documentation on customer lifecycle goals for details on how the adjustment appears in reporting. For related platform reporting gaps, our guide on why GA4 and Google Ads conversions do not match covers the broader attribution picture.

When to use this goal (and when not to)

This goal makes sense when:

  • Your average customer lifetime value materially exceeds the first order value and you have data to support the assumption.
  • You are deliberately investing in acquisition and are willing to judge success on new customer volume and long-run margin, not short-run ROAS.
  • Your Customer Match list is healthy, with 1,000 or more matched customers, so Google's new-customer detection is reliable.
  • You have updated your tROAS target to compensate for the inflation the goal introduces.

It makes less sense when:

  • You are heading into Q4 and need clean ROAS visibility to protect margins on promotional pricing.
  • Your Customer Match list is thin or stale, because Google over-identifies new customers and the adjustment inflates beyond its intended scope.
  • You switched it on recently without raising your tROAS target to account for the adjustment.
  • You are earlier in your advertising journey, with fewer than 50 conversions per month, where Smart Bidding does not yet have enough signal to distinguish new from returning buyers reliably.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Google Ads new customer acquisition goal ROAS?

The new customer acquisition goal adds a value bonus to conversions attributed to new buyers. This bonus is included in the Conversion value column and in the ROAS figure Google reports. It inflates reported ROAS above actual cash return by an amount that depends on the multiplier you set and the share of orders coming from new customers.

Does the Google Ads new customer acquisition goal actually increase revenue?

Not directly. It changes how much you are willing to pay for new customers by adding their estimated long-run value into the bidding signal. Whether that generates more cash revenue depends on whether your new customers actually retain and whether you have set the multiplier to reflect real lifetime value data.

How do I turn off the new customer value multiplier in Performance Max?

Go to your Performance Max campaign settings, find Customer acquisition, and either set the goal to "Do not optimise for new customers" or reduce the new customer value to reflect actual lifetime value. Remove the goal entirely if you need clean ROAS reporting for a peak period.

What is the difference between new customer value mode and new customer only mode?

In value mode, the campaign bids more for new customers but still accepts returning buyers. In new customer only mode, Google bids exclusively on auctions it predicts will produce new customers. The latter suits dedicated acquisition campaigns rather than your main revenue-driving Performance Max.

Why does my Google Ads ROAS not match Shopify?

Several reasons exist, but if you have the new customer acquisition goal active, the value adjustment is a common cause. Google's Conversion value column includes the goal uplift. Shopify reports actual order revenue. Compare using the Original conversion value column in Google Ads, not the headline Conversion value figure.
