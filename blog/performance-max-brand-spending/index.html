Performance Max Spending on Your Brand Name: How to Fix It and What You Risk If You Don't

Performance Max spending on your brand name: how to add brand exclusions, stop ROAS inflation from cheap branded clicks, and protect non-brand growth.

JBy Jack Goldsmith · Published 5 November 2026 · 6 min read

ContentsWhy Performance Max Gravitates to Brand SearchesHow Brand Bidding Inflates Your ROAS Without Growing OrdersHow to Add Brand Exclusions to Performance MaxThe Shopping Ads ExceptionWhat to Expect After Applying Brand ExclusionsWhen Brand Exclusions Are Not the AnswerFrequently Asked Questions

Is Performance Max spending on your brand name and showing impressive ROAS numbers while actual new orders barely move? You are not alone.

Performance Max spends on brand names because Smart Bidding looks for the highest-probability conversion opportunities, and branded searches convert at two to four times the rate of non-brand queries. The fix is brand exclusions: go to Campaign Settings, open Additional settings, select Brand exclusions, add your brand name, and save. This pushes branded demand to a dedicated brand Search campaign, strips PMax of its easiest conversions, and forces the algorithm to earn revenue from customers who do not already know you.

By Jack Goldsmith, Founder & Performance Marketer, Social Surge · 5 November 2026

In the accounts we manage, PMax ROAS inflation from brand cannibalisation is one of the most reliable stuck-growth patterns we see. The campaign reports 18x, 22x, even 30x. Orders are flat. The gap between platform ROAS and actual business growth almost always traces back to branded query volume flowing through PMax unchecked. This post explains why it happens, how to fix it, and what a realistic post-fix performance profile looks like.

Why Performance Max Gravitates to Brand Searches

Performance Max campaigns have no concept of "easy" versus "hard" conversions. Smart Bidding simply targets the highest-probability conversion opportunities within your budget and tROAS target. Branded searches rank near the top of that probability scale: someone who types your brand name into Google has often visited your site before, may have an item in their basket, and trusts you enough to seek you out by name. CPCs on those queries are low because competition is minimal. For the algorithm, this is low-risk, high-return work.

The share of PMax budget going to branded queries depends on your brand awareness relative to your product category. For well-established brands, it can represent the dominant portion of reported conversions. For newer brands with limited branded search volume, the effect is smaller but the data distortion is still there, and it compounds over time as the algorithm learns that branded queries are "good" signals.

This is not a bug in PMax. It is the algorithm doing exactly what it is told: maximise conversion value within the constraints you set. The constraint it is missing is "do not take credit for customers who already know us". That constraint is what brand exclusions add.

How Brand Bidding Inflates Your ROAS Without Growing Orders

The fundamental problem is that branded conversions would mostly have happened anyway. Someone typing your brand name into Google was not discovered by PMax: they already found you through word of mouth, a previous visit, social media, or a recommendation. PMax intercepted a click they were going to make regardless, and then booked the resulting revenue as a campaign win.

The maths makes this look spectacular. If branded queries convert at 12% and non-brand queries convert at 2%, a campaign mixing both reports a blended ROAS far above what non-brand performance alone would justify. That figure then drives scaling decisions: "ROAS is 20x, let's double the budget." Doubling spend into branded demand does not double orders. It mostly doubles the number of clicks from people who already knew where they were going.

In the accounts we manage, the clearest signal is a PMax campaign with ROAS above 15x on a brand with relatively modest awareness. Cross-referencing the Search Insights report inside PMax typically shows branded query categories accounting for a disproportionate share of reported conversions. If you are seeing high PMax ROAS alongside flat order growth, this is the first place to look before you adjust your tROAS target or scale budget.

How to Add Brand Exclusions to Performance Max (Step by Step)

Brand exclusions are the official mechanism for controlling this. They are campaign-level settings applied via a brand list, not keyword negatives, and they apply to Search, Shopping, and YouTube search inventory. Display, video, Discover, Gmail and Maps are not covered by brand exclusions. Here is the process, as documented in Google Ads Help: Brand exclusions for Performance Max:

  1. In Google Ads, go to Campaigns and open your Performance Max campaign.
  2. Click Settings, then scroll to Additional settings.
  3. Click Brand exclusions.
  4. Click New brand list (or select an existing one) and type your brand name. Google matches the brand entity, covering standard misspellings automatically. The brand must exist in Google's brand database.
  5. Save.

For new campaigns, the same section appears under More settings during the campaign creation flow. You can also apply exclusions in bulk via Campaigns > Settings > select campaigns > Edit > Change brands.

Limits to be aware of: up to 10 brand lists per campaign, up to 5,000 brands per list. For most e-commerce stores, one list covering your brand and any close misspellings Google does not auto-match is sufficient.

The Shopping Ads Exception: When to Keep Branded Products On

Adding a brand exclusion stops PMax from serving text ads and YouTube search ads on branded queries. It also removes branded Shopping ads by default. If you want your products to continue appearing in Shopping results when someone searches your brand name, tick the checkbox labelled "Allow Shopping ads on searches that mention excluded brands" before saving.

For most UK e-commerce stores, this split is the right setup:

  • Branded text ads route to a dedicated brand Search campaign where you control the messaging, landing page and bid.
  • Branded Shopping ads continue in PMax (or a feed-only PMax campaign) so your products appear for branded product searches.
  • Non-brand demand is handled by PMax without branded noise inflating the ROAS figure.

Do not apply the exclusion until a funded brand Search campaign is live. Removing branded traffic from PMax without a campaign to catch it may leave that demand unserved or push it to a competitor. If you need help setting up a brand campaign before making this change, our Google Ads management service covers this as part of onboarding.

What to Expect After Applying Brand Exclusions

PMax ROAS will fall. Typically by 20-50% in the first few weeks, depending on how much branded volume was flowing through the campaign. This is not a failure: it is the platform signal correcting itself. The question is what happens to account-level orders and revenue.

Branded demand shifts to the brand Search campaign. Total conversions at account level typically hold steady or improve slightly, because brand Search campaigns tend to be cheaper per click and simpler to control than a generalised PMax asset group. PMax bidding improves over time because Smart Bidding now has a cleaner data set: the conversions it optimises for are genuinely non-brand opportunities the algorithm had to earn.

Give the new setup four to six weeks before drawing conclusions. During that period, judge performance on account-level revenue and order volume rather than on the PMax campaign ROAS figure alone. If you are also planning to scale spend during this period, read our guide on how to scale Google Ads without losing ROAS first; changing budget and campaign structure at the same time makes it hard to identify what is driving any change.

When Brand Exclusions Are Not the Answer

If your brand has minimal search volume (fewer than 100 branded queries per month), brand exclusions are still worth applying for data cleanliness, but the ROAS impact will be small. The overhead of maintaining a separate brand Search campaign may not be justified at very low volumes.

If you are running PMax primarily as a prospecting tool on a brand with very low awareness, the algorithm has little branded traffic to absorb anyway. Exclusions become more important as brand search volume grows, typically once a brand is established enough to appear in Google's brand database.

If PMax ROAS looks high but order volume is growing in line with spend increases and account-level revenue reflects it, the brand inflation may be modest. Run the account-level comparison (platform ROAS versus your Shopify or WooCommerce order count) before making any changes. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I stop Performance Max from bidding on my brand name?

Add a brand exclusion list to the campaign. In Google Ads, go to Campaign Settings, open Additional settings, and click Brand exclusions. Create a new brand list with your brand name and save. If you also want branded Shopping ads to keep running, tick "Allow Shopping ads on searches that mention excluded brands". Have a funded brand Search campaign live before you apply the exclusion, so that branded demand has somewhere to go.

Will my ROAS drop after I add brand exclusions to Performance Max?

Yes, typically by 20-50% in the first few weeks depending on how much branded volume was flowing through the campaign. This is expected and healthy: it means PMax is now bidding on queries it actually had to earn. Judge success on account-level revenue and order volume, not on the PMax campaign ROAS figure alone.

Can Performance Max still show Shopping ads for my brand after exclusions?

Yes. When you add brand exclusions, tick the "Allow Shopping ads on searches that mention excluded brands" checkbox. PMax continues to show product Shopping ads on branded queries while text ads on those searches route to your dedicated brand Search campaign.

How do I know if Performance Max is inflating ROAS with brand searches?

Cross-reference PMax reported conversions against your Shopify or WooCommerce order count over the same period. If the platform shows strong conversions but order volume has not grown proportionally, branded cannibalisation is a likely cause. You can also check the Search Insights report inside the PMax campaign to see the split between branded and non-branded query categories.
