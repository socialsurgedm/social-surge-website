Why Your Google Ads Are Not Converting: Six Root Causes for E-Commerce Stores

Google Ads not converting is almost always one of six root causes: broken conversion tracking, a bid strategy without enough data, match types pulling in irrelevant searches, a landing page that loses the sale, a thin product feed (for Shopping campaigns), or a daily budget too low to let Smart Bidding function. The fix starts with identifying which one applies to your account.

By Jack Goldsmith, Founder & Performance Marketer, Social Surge · 15 September 2026

Why are my Google Ads not converting?

The most likely culprits are: (1) a broken or duplicated conversion tag that is not recording purchases, (2) a bid strategy that has not yet accumulated the 30-50 monthly conversions Smart Bidding needs to optimise, (3) broad match keywords pulling in irrelevant search queries, (4) a landing page that does not match the ad's promise, (5) a Shopping feed with weak titles or missing attributes, or (6) a daily budget so low that the algorithm is throttled before it can find buyers. Diagnose tracking first before changing campaigns.

Getting clicks and no sales is one of the most frustrating situations in e-commerce. It looks like the ads are working (the platform says so), but the revenue is not following. In the accounts we manage, this situation almost always has a fixable structural cause rather than a fundamental market problem. Here is how to work through each one.

1. Check Conversion Tracking Before You Change Anything Else

This is the single most important step and the one most store owners skip. If your conversion tags are not firing correctly, Google Ads will show zero or very low conversions even when sales are happening. Smart Bidding will then assume your ads are failing and stop bidding competitively, creating a self-reinforcing spiral of poor results.

Start by opening Google Tag Assistant and placing a test order on your own site. Confirm the purchase conversion fires once (not twice) and passes a conversion value. Then cross-reference: take a 30-day window and compare Google Ads reported conversions against your Shopify, WooCommerce or platform order count for paid traffic. A gap of more than 10-15% points to a tracking problem.

Common errors we find on new onboardings: the thank-you page fires a conversion tag on every page load rather than once per session; the GA4 import is double-counting alongside a direct Google tag; or enhanced conversions are not enabled, causing conversions to drop out under iOS privacy restrictions. Our Google Ads conversion tracking guide covers the correct setup in detail.

2. Your Smart Bidding Strategy Does Not Have Enough Data

Target ROAS and Target CPA are the workhorses of e-commerce Google Ads, but they require volume to function. Google's own guidance recommends at least 30-50 conversions per month at campaign level before Smart Bidding can reliably predict the next conversion. Below that threshold, the algorithm is effectively guessing.

For newer stores or smaller budgets, this creates a real dilemma: you need conversions to unlock Smart Bidding, but Smart Bidding is supposed to get you conversions. The practical answer is to start on Maximise Conversions (no ROAS target) until you have built the data floor, then layer in a target once the history is there. Setting a Target ROAS of 10x on a campaign with eight conversions a month is asking Smart Bidding to optimise for something it cannot see.

The learning period after launching or making major changes is typically 60-90 days. If your account is genuinely new, what looks like "not converting" is often just the data-gathering phase. See our guide on Google Ads bidding strategy for e-commerce for the full framework.

3. Irrelevant Searches Are Spending Your Budget

Broad match keywords, and Performance Max campaigns without strong audience signals, will frequently match searches that have nothing to do with what you sell. A cycling accessories store can end up serving ads for "cycling holidays" or "cycling statistics GCSE". Each irrelevant click costs money, produces no sale and feeds Smart Bidding bad signal.

Pull your Search Terms report (Campaigns > Insights and reports > Search terms) and filter to the last 30 days. Look for patterns: generic informational queries, competitor brand names if you are not deliberately targeting them, and categories that are adjacent to your products but unlikely to convert. Add negatives systematically. For Performance Max campaigns, adding negative keywords requires a support request or using the account-level negative keyword list, which applies to PMAX automatically.

If a high proportion of your spend is going to irrelevant terms, that alone can explain a conversion rate that looks broken. The clicks are real; the buyers are not there.

4. Your Landing Page Is Where the Sale Happens, Not Your Ads

An ad earns the click. The landing page earns the sale. They are two separate conversion jobs, and it is very common to see well-written ads sending traffic to pages that are not ready to close it.

The most common landing page problems we see: the ad promotes a specific product but sends traffic to a category page, making the customer hunt; the mobile experience is slow (Google's Core Web Vitals data confirms that most UK e-commerce traffic arrives on mobile), the page lacks trust signals such as reviews, returns policy and delivery times; or the price or offer has changed since the ad was written.

Test this: find your highest-spend, lowest-conversion ads and click through yourself on mobile. Is the experience seamless? Does the page match what the ad said? Would you buy from it? Fix the obvious gaps before assuming the ads themselves are broken.

5. Shopping Campaigns: Feed Quality Is Doing the Damage

If your non-converting campaigns include Google Shopping or Performance Max with a feed, the feed itself is often the culprit. Google's Shopping algorithm matches your products to search queries primarily using your product titles and descriptions, not your bids. A poorly titled product with vague attributes will match low-intent searches and be outbid on high-intent ones.

The quick audit: check Merchant Center for disapproved products (each disapproval removes a product from eligibility entirely), then review title structure. Titles should lead with the key attribute buyers search for: brand, product type, model, size, colour. "Blue Shimano Road Bike Groupset 105 R7000 22-Speed" outperforms "Bike Groupset (Blue)" every time. Our Google Shopping feed optimisation guide covers the full title framework and custom label setup.

6. Budget Constraints Are Throttling Smart Bidding

A daily budget set far below your target CPA creates a fundamental mismatch. If your target CPA is £40 and your daily budget is £15, Smart Bidding will struggle to complete even one conversion per day within the budget constraint, and Google will throttle impression delivery before it finds a high-intent buyer.

A practical starting rule: set your daily budget at 5-10 times your target CPA. For a £40 target CPA, that means a daily budget of £200-£400 to give the algorithm room to operate. If budget constraints are flagged on your campaigns (look for the "Limited by budget" label in campaign view), that label is telling you Smart Bidding is being cut off mid-auction. Either increase the budget or reduce the target CPA to match what you can actually spend.

Budget allocation across campaign types is covered in our guide to scaling e-commerce ad budgets.

When the Diagnosis Points to a Deeper Structural Problem

The six causes above cover the vast majority of "Google Ads not converting" situations. If you have worked through all six and are still not seeing traction after a full 60-90 day period, the issue is usually one of three things: account structure is too fragmented to accumulate signal, the niche has genuinely low search demand and a different channel mix is needed, or audience and bidding signals need a full rebuild from scratch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are my Google Ads getting clicks but no conversions?

The most common cause is a tracking gap: clicks are recorded but purchase conversions are not firing correctly. The second most common is poor ad-to-landing-page congruence, where the ad promises something the page does not deliver. Check your conversion tag in Google Tag Assistant and compare Google Ads conversion data against your platform order count for the same period.

How many clicks does it take to get a conversion on Google Ads for ecommerce?

For UK ecommerce, a typical conversion rate is 1-3%, meaning 33-100 clicks per transaction is normal. Premium or high-consideration products (above £200-£300 average order value) may need 80-150 clicks per sale. If you are seeing far higher click counts with zero conversions, the problem is usually tracking, audience relevance or your landing page, not volume.

How long should I wait before deciding Google Ads are not working?

Smart Bidding needs a minimum of 30-50 conversions per month to optimise reliably. For new campaigns, allow 60-90 days before drawing conclusions. The first four to six weeks are a data-gathering phase, not a performance phase, and premature changes reset the learning period and extend the wait.

Should I pause Google Ads if they are not converting?

Pause only if you have confirmed tracking is correct and the account is genuinely wasting budget with no commercial return after 60-90 days. Pausing too early discards conversion data that Smart Bidding needs. More often the right move is to diagnose first: check tracking, search terms, landing pages and bid strategy thresholds before switching anything off.
