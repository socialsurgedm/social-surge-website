Google Ads Performance Max Limited by Budget: What It Means and How to Fix It

When PMax shows "limited by budget", the real problem is often not the budget ceiling itself. It is where the spend is going.

By Jack Goldsmith, Founder & Performance Marketer, Social Surge · 25 October 2026

What does "Performance Max limited by budget" mean?

It means your daily budget cap is constraining what the campaign can spend. For e-commerce stores, this status is more serious than it looks: a budget-constrained PMax campaign often shifts delivery toward cheaper channels such as Display and Gmail rather than the Shopping and Search inventory that drives revenue. The fix is rarely just increasing the budget. It requires auditing target ROAS, conversion data volume, feed quality and, since October 2026, applying Google's new channel prioritisation and budget allocation controls.

Performance Max limited by budget is one of the more misleading campaign status labels in Google Ads. It sounds like a simple spending problem. In practice, it is usually a signal that the algorithm is allocating your capped budget across the wrong mix of channels, concentrating spend where clicks are cheapest rather than where purchase intent is highest. This guide covers what is actually happening inside a budget-constrained PMax campaign, why it behaves differently to Search, and the step-by-step process we use in the accounts we manage to resolve it without wasting spend.

What "Performance Max Limited by Budget" Actually Means

When Google Ads shows a campaign as "limited by budget", it means the campaign is on track to spend its daily cap before the day ends, or is already doing so. For a standard Search campaign, that is a straightforward trade-off: you are missing some impression share because the budget runs dry. For Performance Max, the signal is more complicated.

PMax is an automated campaign type that runs simultaneously across Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail and Maps. Google's algorithm allocates spend across those channels in real time, chasing the conversion signals it has learned from your account data. When budget is tight, the algorithm concentrates delivery on whatever inventory it predicts will convert at the lowest cost, not the highest revenue. For e-commerce campaigns with a target ROAS above a certain threshold, that often means cheaper Display and Gmail placements win budget over competitive Shopping auctions.

Why PMax Handles Budget Constraints Differently to Search

In a Search campaign, "limited by budget" simply means some search queries went unserved. In PMax, the same constraint triggers a prioritisation cascade across multiple networks simultaneously.

Shopping CPCs are typically higher than Display CPCs in the same account. When a PMax campaign is constrained, the algorithm can hit its daily cap faster by concentrating on Display (where each click costs less) than on Shopping (where auction competition pushes CPCs higher). The result is a campaign that looks budget-efficient in the platform interface but is quietly reducing its share of spend on the high-intent product inventory that generates actual revenue.

In the accounts we manage, we routinely see budget-constrained PMax campaigns reporting a healthy blended ROAS while Shopping impression share sits well below its unconstrained level. The aggregate numbers look acceptable. The revenue opportunity is not being captured.

Why Display Can Eat Your Shopping Budget

Three factors combine to push a constrained PMax campaign toward Display and away from Shopping:

  • Competitive Shopping CPCs. Shopping inventory is contested by every retailer in your product category. Display rarely faces the same auction pressure, so clicks are cheaper and the algorithm can buy more of them within a tight daily cap.
  • Target ROAS as a throttle. If your tROAS target is set higher than what recent account data can support, Smart Bidding deprioritises expensive auctions it calculates it cannot win at your target. Shopping placements are often the first casualty because they carry higher CPCs.
  • Thin conversion data. PMax needs roughly 30-50 conversions per month to allocate budget efficiently across channels. Below that threshold, the algorithm leans on cheaper, easier-to-win inventory because it lacks the signal to bid confidently on competitive Shopping placements.

These factors can stack. A campaign with a high tROAS target, thin conversion volume and a capped daily budget will almost always migrate spend toward Display. The "limited by budget" status describes the symptom; the causes sit underneath it.

The New Channel Prioritisation Controls: What Changed in October 2026

Google rolled out channel prioritisation and budget allocation controls for Performance Max from mid-October 2026 (alongside Search, Shopping and Demand Gen campaigns). This is directly relevant to the allocation problem described above.

The controls let you assign channel groups to a High or Low priority tier. For Low-priority channels, you can also set a maximum percentage of daily budget they are allowed to consume. Practical applications for e-commerce:

  • Set Display Network to Low priority with a 0% cap and Google will not spend there at all.
  • Set Search Partners to Low priority with a 10% cap and they can consume at most 10% of the daily budget before the remainder flows to higher-priority placements.
  • Leave the main Google channels group (Search, Shopping, YouTube, Maps, Gmail, Discover) on High priority so that Shopping and Search are funded ahead of partner networks.

There is a significant limitation worth knowing upfront: Shopping, Search, YouTube, Maps, Gmail and Discover all sit inside one "Google channels" bucket. You cannot rank Shopping above YouTube using these controls alone. If YouTube is consuming a constrained budget at the expense of Shopping, channel prioritisation will not separate them. The options in that scenario are a tighter tROAS, cleaner primary conversion goals, or splitting demand across a feed-only PMax campaign and a Standard Shopping campaign.

To apply the settings: go to account settings and find Channel prioritisation, or override at the campaign level under Settings. The full technical reference is in Google Ads Help: channel prioritisation and budget allocation. Google's October 2026 announcement covers the rollout timeline and account eligibility.

Why Performance Max Is Not Spending Even When Limited by Budget

This is a specific and common pattern: the campaign shows "limited by budget" in the status column but finishes the day well under its cap. The cause is almost always a tROAS target that is out of reach.

When your target ROAS is set above what the algorithm can currently achieve, Smart Bidding throttles delivery rather than bidding into auctions it calculates it cannot win at your target. The campaign triggers the "limited by budget" label because at some projected spending rate it would reach the cap; but it never actually gets there. You see the status and assume you need more budget. The real problem is a ROAS target that needs recalibrating.

Our guide on Google Ads target ROAS not working covers the diagnostic in detail. The short version: if your actual ROAS over the last 30 days is more than 15-20% below your target, reduce the target in increments of 10-15% and hold each change for 7-14 days before reassessing. Do not cut tROAS by 50% in one move; it forces Smart Bidding through an unnecessary re-learning phase.

The Fix: A Step-by-Step Approach

Work through this sequence in order before increasing your daily budget cap:

1. Check channel-level performance

In Google Ads, go to Insights and reports, then Channel performance (data available from mid-2025 onwards). Review cost, conversions and conversion value split by channel. If Display or YouTube accounts for a disproportionate share of spend relative to its conversion value, you have a channel allocation problem. Increasing the budget will replicate that problem at higher cost.

2. Apply channel prioritisation controls

Set Display Network to Low priority with a 0% or low percentage cap. If Search Partners are diluting budget with weak return, set those to Low with a 10% or lower cap. This directs more of your constrained daily budget toward Shopping and Search inventory. Test on one campaign first before applying account-level defaults, so you do not shift every PMax campaign at once.

3. Audit your target ROAS

Pull the last 30 days of campaign data and compare actual ROAS to your tROAS target. If actual is more than 15-20% below target, bring the target within range and allow 7-14 days for Smart Bidding to recalibrate. This step alone often resolves the "limited by budget but not spending" pattern described above.

4. Check conversion volume

If the campaign is producing fewer than 30 conversions in the last 30 days, efficient channel allocation is not possible regardless of settings. See our guide on how much to spend on Google Ads for e-commerce for the minimum spend levels needed to clear the conversion threshold across different average order values.

5. Review product feed quality

Missing GTINs, generic product titles and low-quality images reduce Shopping auction eligibility directly. A budget-constrained campaign with a weak product feed will default to Display almost every time, because those placements do not require feed data to serve. Our Google Shopping feed optimisation guide covers the specific attributes that move eligibility and auction volume.

6. Only then consider a budget increase

If channel allocation is corrected, tROAS is within range of actual account performance, conversion volume is above the threshold and feed quality is solid, a staged budget increase of 15-20% is a reasonable next step. Hold each increment for at least 14 days before making another change. If those conditions are not met, a larger budget will replicate the same allocation problems at higher cost.

For accounts where YouTube continues to compete with Shopping even after tROAS adjustments, the hybrid structure (a feed-only PMax campaign running alongside a Standard Shopping campaign) gives Shopping inventory a dedicated budget and provides an independent performance benchmark. Performance Max vs Standard Shopping covers when this structure is worth the added management overhead.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I just increase my PMax budget if it shows limited by budget?

Not automatically. First check that your target ROAS is within 10-15% of actual account performance, that you have at least 30-50 conversions per month, and that your product feed is healthy. If those conditions are not met, a larger budget will typically produce more Display and YouTube spend rather than Shopping revenue. Only increase budget once the underlying data quality is in place.

What are the new Google Ads channel prioritisation controls for Performance Max?

From October 2026, Google rolled out channel prioritisation and budget allocation controls for PMax. You can set channels to High or Low priority and add a percentage spend cap. Display Network and Search Partners can be set to Low priority with a 0% cap to stop spend there entirely. However, you cannot separate Shopping from YouTube within the main Google channels bucket; both sit under one group.

Why is my Performance Max campaign limited by budget but not spending?

Target ROAS is the most common cause. When your tROAS target is set higher than what the algorithm can achieve, Smart Bidding throttles delivery rather than bidding into auctions it calculates it cannot win at your target. The campaign triggers the limited by budget status but never actually reaches its daily cap. Bringing tROAS within 10-15% of your actual recent performance usually restores normal delivery.

How do I know if Display is eating my Performance Max Shopping budget?

Open Insights and reports, then Channel performance. Check cost, conversions and conversion value split by channel. If Display or YouTube is consuming a large share of spend with low conversion value while Shopping impression share is low, channel dilution is the likely issue. Use the new channel prioritisation controls to deprioritise Display, or run a separate feed-only PMax campaign alongside Standard Shopping to force more spend toward product inventory.
