Google Ads Smart Bidding Exploration Lowering ROAS

Google Ads Smart Bidding Exploration lowering ROAS is the tolerance on the slider, not a broken auction. A 5-30% cut becomes the target the bidder actually uses.

JBy Jack Goldsmith · Published 26 November 2026 · 6 min read

Google Ads Smart Bidding Exploration lowering ROAS is not a fault in the auction. It is the tolerance you set, from 5% to 30%, which cuts the target the bidder actually uses. Google's example is a 200% target with a 10% tolerance, which becomes an effective target of 180%. You agreed to that lower return so it can bid higher on long-tail queries the tight target was skipping.

By Jack Goldsmith, Founder & Performance Marketer, Social Surge · 26 November 2026

In the accounts we manage, settings still show the original target the next morning. Black Friday 2026 is 27 November. If you lift spend tomorrow against that field, you are scaling a bid already 5-30% looser than it.

Why Performance Max explores below target ROAS

Performance Max exploring below target ROAS happens when Smart Bidding Exploration is on and the campaign bids to target ROAS. Google's current guide says a feed campaign uses one toggle for Search and Shopping. With no feed, exploration is Search only. The lower target is the feature, not a glitch in the product ads.

An older Performance Max article still says feed campaigns, and shop-goal campaigns, are not eligible. If the setting is missing, that older rule is the one you are on. Do not assume Shopping is under target because a Search campaign in the same account is exploring.

Exploration does not add keywords or widen who can see the ad. Google's line is that targeting is the pond and bidding is the rod: the pond stays the same size. Cutting the target yourself tends to lift bids across matched queries. Exploration is meant to bid higher only on selected slices, so the gain is more distinct search categories, not a blanket increase.

If the campaign is limited by budget, Google says exploration is disabled in the background until the limit lifts. Clearing that status in sale week can switch the looser bid back on as you add spend. Portfolio bidding works with exploration on Search only. Move Shopping or Performance Max onto a portfolio and it switches off. If ROAS fell and the campaign is in a portfolio, look at the sale price, the feed, and whether target ROAS was already missing.

A Shopping campaign accepting a lower ROAS before Black Friday

A Shopping campaign accepting a lower ROAS before Black Friday is often two cuts at once. The tolerance has already lowered the effective target, and the sale price lowers conversion value on the same click. Google says exploration is compatible with seasonality adjustments. Compatible does not mean the cash return holds. The bidder spends toward the lower target while the basket is cheaper.

A 400% target (4x) with a 30% tolerance becomes 280% (2.8x). That is the same rule as Google's 200% to 180% example: 30% lower than 400% is 280%, not 370%. Google says actual ROAS should come within about 15% of the effective target over one to two conversion cycles, after at least 50 conversions. That is their window, not a promise.

If break-even after the sale price and the cost of goods sits above that effective target, the campaign can hit the loosened number and still lose money. Do not add budget until you have done that sum. Do not switch exploration on tonight. Google wants at least six weeks before you judge it. There is no formal learning period, but the ramp can take one to two weeks.

Gift queries in cycling, fishing and outdoor catalogues are the long tail this feature bids harder on. Useful in a normal month. A poor week to find a helmet or jacket bidding at 2.8x after the promotion has taken the margin.

Where the bidder's target actually sits

The target field does not rewrite itself. Change history records when exploration was turned on, so check the log before you blame the sale, the feed or tracking. Open the bid strategy report. Google adds a timeline note for the dates it was active, and it wants traffic diversity judged there: unique search categories with impressions, clicks and conversions. If diversity has not moved and cost has, you are paying the tolerance without the trade.

Ad group targets are not a shelter. A 10% tolerance lowers ad group overrides by 10% as well. Google does not recommend CPC bid limits while exploration is on, because the cap limits the traffic it can take. A ceiling added so the sale cannot run away can blunt the feature and leave the lower target in place.

Should you raise target ROAS to offset it?

No. Google says raising the target to cancel the tolerance limits how much extra traffic it can treat as worth a bid. Do not edit the target in the week you turn the feature on, and do not edit it on Friday morning to match a forecast.

If you want the original efficiency back, turn exploration off. Google says Smart Bidding keeps what it learned, so this is not a wipe. It is cleaner than a target you must remember to undo on Monday, when sale returns move ROAS for a different reason. Do not stack a seasonality adjustment, a budget increase and a target edit before the doors open. If the bidding strategy was already wrong, fix it after the weekend.

When not to raise the budget

Leave the daily budget where it is if any of these are true.

  • There is no ROAS target. Exploration is not available on maximise conversion value without one. A soft ROAS there is a different fault.
  • Fewer than 50 conversions since it went on. You cannot separate noise, delay and the tolerance.
  • The campaign is limited by budget. Exploration may already be off. Raising the cap can switch it back on during the sale.
  • Break-even sits above the effective target once the sale price and the cost of goods are in. The Black Friday budget piece covers scaling Shopping without treating sale-week ROAS as the full-price one.
  • Shopping or Performance Max sits in a portfolio. Exploration is off. Look elsewhere.

Our published average is 18.3x ROAS, with floors of 13x and above in cycling, 12x and above in fishing and 14x and above in outdoor, and a best month of 35.3x. That is not a forecast for a campaign told to bid up to 30% under target into a discount. Google Ads management is £1,500 a month. Fees are on the pricing page, results on case studies. Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Google Ads Smart Bidding Exploration lowering ROAS?

Google Ads Smart Bidding Exploration lowering ROAS is the tolerance you set, from 5% to 30%. Google's example is a 200% target with a 10% tolerance, which becomes an effective target of 180%. The bidder uses that lower number so it can bid higher on long-tail queries you already qualify for. It does not add keywords or widen who can see the ad.

Why is Performance Max exploring below target ROAS?

Performance Max exploring below target ROAS means Smart Bidding Exploration is on and the campaign uses target ROAS. With a Merchant Center feed, Google's current guide says one toggle covers Search and Shopping. Without a feed, exploration applies to Search only. An older Performance Max article still says feed campaigns and shop goals are not eligible, so if the setting is missing, Shopping is not on this feature.

Why is my Shopping campaign accepting a lower ROAS before Black Friday?

A Shopping campaign accepting a lower ROAS before Black Friday is usually the tolerance plus the sale price. The effective target is already 5-30% under the number in settings, and a discount cuts conversion value on the same click. Google says to judge it after at least 50 conversions and about six weeks, not on the morning of the sale. Do not turn it on the night before Black Friday.

Should I raise target ROAS to offset Smart Bidding Exploration?

No. Google says raising the target to offset exploration limits the extra queries it can bid on. If you want the old target back, turn exploration off rather than editing the target on the same day you raise the budget. Ad group targets are lowered by the same percentage, and a campaign that is limited by budget may already have exploration paused.
