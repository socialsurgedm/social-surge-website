How Long Does Google Ads Take to Work for E-Commerce Stores?

How long does Google Ads take to work for e-commerce? The 60-90 day Smart Bidding timeline, conversion thresholds and what slows results for UK stores.

How long does Google Ads take to work for e-commerce?

Most e-commerce accounts need 60-90 days before Smart Bidding has enough data to optimise reliably. The first month is data collection; performance typically stabilises in weeks four to eight; by day 90 you should have a clear ROAS signal. Campaigns that cannot reach 30-50 conversions per month stay in an extended learning phase and rarely deliver consistent results without structural changes.

Google Ads for e-commerce does not produce reliable results on day one. The 60-90 day timeline is not an agency excuse; it reflects how Smart Bidding actually works. The algorithm needs real purchase conversion data from your account before it can make accurate bid decisions, and that data takes time to accumulate. What you do in those first 90 days (budget level, structural choices, conversion tracking accuracy) determines whether the algorithm learns the right things or drifts in entirely the wrong direction.

Why Does Google Ads Take Time to Work?

Modern Google Ads campaigns run on Smart Bidding: automated strategies including Target ROAS, Maximise Conversion Value, and Performance Max all rely on it. The algorithm predicts which auctions are worth bidding up on by analysing the conversion patterns specific to your account. Without sufficient data, it makes statistically weak predictions, and early bid decisions reflect that uncertainty.

Google's own Smart Bidding documentation references a learning period after significant account changes, during which performance can be volatile. The conversion volume that makes Smart Bidding reliable is typically 30-50 purchase conversions per month at campaign level. In the accounts we manage, we use 30 purchases within a 30-day window as the floor before moving from Maximise Conversions to a Target ROAS strategy. Below that volume, ROAS numbers are statistically noisy and should not drive structural decisions.

Accounts that are frequently adjusted (bid strategy switches, large budget cuts, campaign restructures) repeatedly reset the learning phase. This is one of the most common reasons accounts never settle: they are perpetually re-learning rather than optimising.

Month 1: Data Collection, Not Results

For a brand-new Google Ads account, the first 30 days are primarily about data collection. Smart Bidding starts from zero, exploring different query types, audiences, and placements to discover what converts for your store. During this window you should expect:

  • Cost-per-click (CPC) variation as the algorithm tests the auction landscape
  • Fluctuating impression share while quality scores and relevance signals are established
  • Early ROAS that looks nothing like where you will eventually land

This is not wasted time. Month one is the most important window for structural work: confirming that purchase conversion tracking is firing correctly (broken tracking means the algorithm is learning from noise rather than signal), building your negative keyword list from early search term reports, and ensuring your Shopping feed has complete attributes and GTINs. Problems caught here are cheap to fix; the same problems at month three have already distorted your bidding data.

Budget matters at this stage too. A daily budget of £15-20 on competitive UK purchase queries will rarely generate 30 conversions per month. If you want to understand the minimum budget needed to make Smart Bidding viable, our post on how much to spend on Google Ads for e-commerce covers the numbers in detail. We also work through this during every free PPC audit, as budget-to-threshold mismatch is one of the most common reasons new accounts stall.

Months Two and Three: When Performance Starts to Settle

Weeks four to eight are typically where performance begins to stabilise. Smart Bidding has accumulated meaningful auction data, your negative keyword list is filtering irrelevant traffic, and quality scores have found their level. In the accounts we manage, the period around weeks eight to ten is usually when we have a reliable read on whether a campaign will deliver at the expected ROAS.

Before that point, results can mislead in both directions. A campaign can look poor when the algorithm is simply still learning, or it can look deceptively strong in the early weeks only to settle lower once seasonal patterns and budget changes test the model properly. This is why an 8-10 week review window is far more meaningful than a 2-3 week one, and why agencies promising meaningful ROAS results inside the first fortnight are either measuring the wrong things or declaring victory too early.

By day 90, a well-structured account with sufficient budget should show: a settled ROAS pattern, predictable daily spend, and search term reports where irrelevant traffic has been largely filtered out. The 60-90 day framing is consistent with Google's own account performance guidance, not an industry invention.

What Slows Google Ads Down (or Stops It Working Entirely)

Several factors extend the 60-90 day timeline significantly, or prevent campaigns from ever reaching reliable performance:

Broken conversion tracking. If purchase events are not recording correctly, Smart Bidding has no valid signal to learn from. This is more common than most store owners realise. Our guide on Google Ads conversion tracking for e-commerce covers the most frequent errors and how to verify your setup using Google's Tag Assistant tool. Accurate tracking is covered in Google's conversion tracking setup documentation.

Insufficient budget. Campaigns that cannot generate 30 conversions per month stay in a permanent learning phase. The only exits are a higher budget, a lower-priced product range to build conversion volume, or a longer patience window before switching bid strategies.

Frequent structural changes. Switching bid strategies, removing large numbers of keywords, or restructuring ad groups resets the learning phase each time. In the accounts we manage, structural changes are planned deliberately and avoided during critical learning windows.

Poor Shopping feed quality. For Performance Max and Standard Shopping campaigns, the product feed is the primary signal used to match products to search queries. Incomplete attributes, missing GTINs, and vague product titles push the algorithm towards weaker predictions. Our post on Google Shopping feed optimisation covers the specific attributes that move ROAS and the routine that keeps them healthy.

Timing in Niche E-Commerce

Timing varies meaningfully by category. In the accounts we manage across cycling, fishing, and outdoor niches, demand follows a seasonal curve. A cycling store launching Google Ads in January will generate fewer monthly conversions than one launching in April, even with identical setup, because purchase intent in the category is lower during off-peak months. That seasonal effect can make a structurally sound campaign look like it is failing when the real issue is timing relative to the selling season.

High average order value (AOV) products also reach conversion thresholds more slowly on a given budget. A product priced at £300-400 generates fewer purchases per £1,000 of ad spend than one at £50-60, meaning the 30-conversion floor takes longer to reach and requires a proportionally higher initial budget commitment. There is no single budget figure that works for every product type; this is something we cover specifically during free audits for stores in our niche verticals. You can also see how we approach it across our case studies.

When to Be Patient and When to Act

Patience has limits. By day 45-60, certain signals indicate a structural problem rather than normal learning delay:

  • Near-zero conversions despite meaningful spend (suggests broken conversion tracking or a budget-to-CPA gap the algorithm cannot bridge)
  • ROAS consistently below 1x past week six (spending more than you are returning for six weeks is a clear intervention signal)
  • Search terms overwhelmingly dominated by irrelevant, non-purchase-intent queries despite active negative keyword management
  • Impression share persistently below 10-15%, pointing to budget or quality problems preventing meaningful auction participation

The 60-90 day timeline is real and documented. Understanding it means you can set up the first three months correctly: giving campaigns the time and structure they need to learn, rather than either pulling the plug on a campaign that just needed more data, or staying passive with one that needed intervention weeks ago. Both mistakes are expensive; knowing which situation you are in is the starting point.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does Google Ads take to work for e-commerce?

Most e-commerce accounts need 60-90 days before Smart Bidding has enough data to optimise reliably. The first month is data collection; performance typically stabilises in weeks four to eight; by day 90 you should have a clear ROAS signal. Campaigns that cannot reach 30-50 conversions per month stay in an extended learning phase and rarely deliver consistent results without structural changes.

Can you speed up the Google Ads learning phase?

Yes, within limits. A higher daily budget reaches conversion thresholds faster, which compresses the timeline. Launching with Maximise Conversions rather than a Target ROAS strategy gives the algorithm more flexibility early on. Avoiding structural changes during the learning window (bid strategy switches, large keyword removals) prevents unnecessary resets. What you cannot compress is the minimum signal density the algorithm needs; patience and correct setup are the main levers.

How many conversions does Google Ads need to work properly?

Google's Smart Bidding performs most reliably with 30-50 purchase conversions per month at campaign level. Below that threshold, the algorithm lacks enough signal density to make accurate bid predictions, and ROAS tends to be unstable. In the accounts we manage, we wait for at least 30 conversions in a 30-day window before switching to a Target ROAS strategy.

Should you keep running Google Ads if results are poor after 30 days?

Generally yes, if the account is structured correctly and has sufficient budget. Poor results at 30 days are normal during the data-collection phase. The signals to watch for at 45-60 days are: near-zero conversions despite meaningful spend (which suggests a tracking problem), ROAS consistently below 1x, or search terms dominated by irrelevant queries. Those are structural issues that require intervention, not simply more time.
