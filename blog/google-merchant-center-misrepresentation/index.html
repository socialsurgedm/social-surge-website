Google Merchant Center Misrepresentation: How to Fix the Suspension and Restore Your Shopping Ads

A misrepresentation suspension stops Shopping and Performance Max ads immediately. Here is why it happens to UK stores, the VAT pricing trap that catches most, and the exact order of fixes that gets an account reinstated.

By Jack Goldsmith, Founder & Performance Marketer, Social Surge · 27 October 2026

What is a Google Merchant Center misrepresentation suspension?

A Google Merchant Center misrepresentation suspension means Google believes your website, product feed or account information does not accurately represent your business to shoppers. It can affect individual products or your entire account, stopping Shopping and Performance Max ads from running until it is resolved. The fix requires auditing and correcting every discrepancy on the live site before submitting a single, well-documented review request.

When a misrepresentation notice lands in your Merchant Center account, Shopping and Performance Max revenue can drop to zero within hours. The instinct is to appeal immediately, but that is usually the wrong move: each failed review can trigger a longer cool-down period, making subsequent reinstatement harder. Work through the fixes first, then appeal once with clear evidence of what changed.

What Google's Misrepresentation Policy Actually Covers

Google's misrepresentation policy exists to protect shoppers from misleading business practices. It covers: inaccurate pricing, misleading returns or cancellation policies, business identity that does not match the site, product claims the business cannot substantiate, and promotional tactics such as fake countdown timers or permanent "was/now" prices that create false urgency or mislead on value.

The suspension is not necessarily permanent, but Google's guidance notes that reinstatement happens only in compelling circumstances. The bar for a successful appeal is that the account looks clean to a Google reviewer arriving at the site cold, in an incognito window, on both desktop and mobile. In the accounts we manage, the cause is almost never deliberate: it is typically a policy page written years ago that contradicts current checkout settings, or a feed exported with ex-VAT prices while the site shows inc-VAT totals.

The Most Common Causes for UK E-Commerce Stores

Business identity discrepancies

Google checks that the legal name, trading name, phone number and contact email on your website match the information in your Merchant Center account and, where relevant, your Google Business Profile. Using a Gmail address as the primary business contact raises flags. So does a mismatch between a Companies House-registered name and what appears in Merchant Center. If you are a limited company, showing your company number on your About page and matching your registration data across all platforms removes this risk category entirely.

Incomplete or contradictory consumer information

Under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, UK distance sellers must display clearly before purchase: the total price including VAT, delivery costs and timescales, the 14-day right to cancel from the date the customer receives goods, who pays return postage for change-of-mind returns, and any exceptions that genuinely apply (personalised items, sealed hygiene goods, and so on). Policy text that is copied from a template, fails to state who pays return postage, or contradicts what happens at checkout is a leading cause of misrepresentation flags. Every required policy page (shipping, returns, privacy and contact) should be linked in the footer of every page, including checkout.

Misleading promotions

Permanent "was/now" pricing where the original price was never genuine, countdown timers that reset on each visit, and fake stock-scarcity messages all fall under misrepresentation. Google crawls product landing pages and compares what shoppers see against what the feed declares. If the two do not match, or if the promotional tactics themselves mislead, a disapproval or suspension is likely.

Conflicting shipping settings

The delivery time and cost shown on your product pages must match the shipping settings configured in Merchant Center. Shopify and WooCommerce stores running multiple shipping apps frequently end up with two sets of rules, only one of which Google reads. Check both the platform settings and the Merchant Center shipping configuration side by side before appealing.

Price Mismatch and VAT: the Most Overlooked Cause

Products disapproved for "price mismatch" or "inaccurate prices (VAT-related issues)" are among the most common Google Shopping feed problems for UK stores, and a large proportion of affected products can escalate to an account-level misrepresentation warning.

Google requires that the price attribute in your feed reflects the total consumer-facing price including VAT, in GBP. The product page price, structured data markup (schema.org) and checkout price must all match that figure exactly. For UK stores selling standard-rated goods, do not use the tax attribute. Google's product data specification confirms the UK is a VAT-inclusive country for feed pricing.

A systematic gap of roughly 20% between feed prices and page prices is almost always a VAT issue. It typically appears when:

  • WooCommerce is configured to store prices excluding tax and the feed plugin pulls the net price
  • Shopify's Google and YouTube channel sends the ex-VAT price rather than the customer-facing inc-VAT price
  • Sale prices are rounded differently in the feed versus the product page

Fix it by setting the feed to export VAT-inclusive GBP prices, updating the schema.org price markup on product pages to match, and confirming that delivery costs are carried in your Merchant Center shipping settings rather than bundled into the price attribute. How to Fix a Misrepresentation Suspension: the Order Matters

Submitting a review before the site is clean wastes the appeal and starts a cool-down timer. Work through these steps in sequence:

  1. Screenshot the suspension notice and every "Needs attention" item in Merchant Center's Diagnostics tab. This creates a record and ensures you address every flagged issue, not just the most obvious one.
  2. Audit the site as a first-time UK shopper. Open an incognito window on desktop and mobile. Check: business identity across the footer, About and Contact pages; all required policy pages linked in the footer; VAT-inclusive pricing with no misleading promotional price claims; delivery information matching your Merchant Center shipping settings; and returns wording that accurately describes the 14-day cancellation right and who pays return postage.
  3. Fix feed price mismatches. Compare feed prices against live product page prices for a representative sample of products. Correct any VAT or rounding discrepancies at feed level and resubmit. If you also have a broader product disapproval issue alongside the misrepresentation notice, clear both before appealing.
  4. Allow time for recrawling. Once fixes are live, use Google Search Console's URL Inspection tool to request re-indexing of key pages. Many successful reinstatements involve a waiting period of around seven days after fixes before the review is submitted.
  5. Submit one review request. In Merchant Center, navigate to the account issue and select "Request review." Choose "I fixed the issue." In the free-text field, list each specific fix with a URL and the date it was made. Keep it factual. Do not argue the original decision and do not submit a second request while one is pending.

Do not open a second Merchant Center account. The suspension follows the business, and a second account is typically suspended immediately.

If the First Review Is Rejected

A rejected review is not final, but it does mean a mandatory waiting period before the next attempt, and each rejection can extend that period. Use the time to look harder: ask a colleague or customer to navigate the site fresh, and compare your returns policy against the GOV.UK distance selling guidance to check that cancellation rights, return postage liability and applicable exceptions are all stated correctly before purchase.

In the accounts we have supported through this process, the item missed most often on the first review is the returns and cancellation wording: specifically, failing to state who pays return postage before the customer places an order. Fixing that single item has been enough to turn a rejected appeal into a successful reinstatement.

If the account has exhausted its review attempts, or the underlying issue is a business model or product category Google will not accept, use Merchant Center's in-product support contact route rather than repeating self-service appeals. When to Seek Outside Help

Most misrepresentation suspensions are fixable without outside help if you work through them methodically. Seek help when:

  • You have already had a review rejected and cannot identify what remains wrong
  • The suspension coincides with a site migration, a new shipping app, or a policy update (a single change can touch multiple settings simultaneously, which is why these situations appear regularly in our experience)
  • The account, rather than individual products, has been suspended and the Diagnostics tab shows no specific policy item to address

A Google Ads management team with direct Merchant Center access can often spot the remaining discrepancy in a single review. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Google Merchant Center misrepresentation suspension?

It is a Google policy violation triggered when your website, product feed or account details are judged to misrepresent your business to shoppers. It can affect individual products or the whole account, and it stops Shopping and Performance Max ads from running until it is resolved.

Why are my products disapproved for price mismatch in Merchant Center?

The most common cause for UK stores is a feed exporting prices excluding VAT while the product page shows prices including VAT. A systematic 20% gap between feed price and page price is usually the tell. Fix the feed to export VAT-inclusive GBP prices, update any schema.org markup on product pages, and resubmit.

Do UK Google Shopping feeds need VAT-inclusive prices?

Yes. Google requires the consumer-facing price in the price attribute, which for UK shoppers means the total including VAT. Do not use the tax attribute for UK targets. The price in the feed, on the product page and in structured data must all match.

How long does a Google Merchant Center misrepresentation review take?

Google typically takes 3-7 business days, though it can take longer. Do not submit a second request while one is pending. If the review is rejected, fix any remaining issues and wait out any cool-down period before appealing again.

Can I open a new Merchant Center account after a misrepresentation suspension?

No. The suspension follows the business, and a second account is typically suspended immediately. Fix the issues in the original account and request a review through the proper in-account appeal process.
