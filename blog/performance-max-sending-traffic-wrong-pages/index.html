Performance Max Sending Traffic to the Wrong Pages

Performance Max sending traffic to the wrong pages means the click landed on a blog, size guide or policy, not the product. Final URL expansion, on by default, is what swaps the URL.

JBy Jack Goldsmith · Published 24 November 2026 · 6 min read

Performance Max sending traffic to the wrong pages means the click never reached the product. It reached a blog post, a size guide, a delivery page or a policy. Final URL expansion is on by default, and it can replace the asset group URL with another page on the same domain.

By Jack Goldsmith, Founder & Performance Marketer, Social Surge · 24 November 2026

In the accounts we manage, spend keeps moving while add to baskets go quiet. The campaign has not stopped working. It is buying a page that cannot take the order. Sort the destination before you touch target ROAS, and before you scale a Black Friday budget that includes those clicks. Black Friday 2026 falls on 27 November.

Why the click leaves the product page

Expansion is not a broken feed. Google can swap the final URL for a page it thinks matches the query. If text customisation is also on, it may rewrite the headline and description to suit that page, and pull images from it. The ad looks deliberate, click-through holds up, and the waste sits inside a campaign that still looks healthy.

Any page you have not excluded can serve. A brand folder is not a fence, so the journal, the sizing chart, the returns policy and last year's gift guide can all take the click. They often rank in organic search and have no add to basket.

A page feed does not stop this. With expansion on, it helps Google index those URLs. It does not limit serving to that list. With expansion off, traffic stays on the asset group URLs and, if you attached one, the page feed. Store-goal campaigns do not support Final URL expansion. Online sales campaigns do.

How to turn off final URL expansion in Google Ads

To turn off final URL expansion in Google Ads, open the campaign, select the gear icon, then Campaign settings, then Asset optimisation. Under Text, clear the Final URL checkbox and save. Leave text customisation on unless the headlines are the problem. If one page is the only path to a sale, that is the combination Google recommends, with the asset group URL set to that page.

Do not turn it off just to feel in control. On a large catalogue it also reaches product URLs you never pasted in. If product pages still take most of the cost, exclude the blogs and policies and leave expansion on. Turn it off when content URLs are most of the cost, or the shop converts on one page. Do not exclude your way down to a URL that redirects, 404s, or blocks Google's ads crawler.

Where the PMax landing page report shows blog spend

The standard Landing pages page will not answer this. Google says it reports URLs from Search, Display, Video and Shopping. Performance Max is not in that list, so a clean report can hide a blog that PMax is funding.

For PMax landing page report blog spend, open Search terms in the Campaigns menu, then Search terms and landing pages for Performance Max. It ties the query to the landing page and the format, Shopping ads or text ads. Data starts in March 2023. Sort by cost, not conversions. Clear a URL because it cannot take the order, not because the click count looks odd.

Under asset reporting, the Expanded final URL assets tab lists the URL expansion swapped in. Removing an asset there removes it for the whole campaign. In the accounts we manage, we filter for blog, guide, size, delivery, returns and policy first.

Shopping ads and text ads are different faults

Split by ad format first. A blog or policy on a text ad is expansion. A Shopping ad on the wrong product is usually the Merchant Center link, and turning expansion off will not rewrite it. Fix that URL in the feed, then come back to expansion.

Exclude the folder, or the homepage still serves

URL exclusions leave expansion on. They are for non-commercial pages: careers, legal, thin content, a range you no longer sell. Under Asset optimisation, choose Add URL exclusions. Paste URLs, or add a rule that contains /blog/ or /size-guide/. A trailing space stops the rule. A normal trailing slash is fine.

You cannot exclude the campaign's own final URL. If the asset group points at the homepage, excluding it does nothing. Point the asset group at a product or category, then exclude the homepage. On a Merchant Center campaign, Google says the homepage may still serve on some inventory, so check the list again after the change.

Exclude the folder, not each new post. Exclusions only apply while expansion is on.

Size guides are not the product

A size guide belongs on the product page as a link, not as the ad URL. In cycling accounts, a helmet query that lands on a measuring guide loses the basket. Outdoor does the same when a tent query hits a pitching article, and fishing when a reel query hits a rig guide. Fine for organic search. A poor paid landing page when the query is a gift and the page has no price.

If the product URL is thin, expansion goes looking. Do not noindex the size guide to fix the ads. Exclude the folder instead. Wider faults sit in why Performance Max is not working.

When not to change target ROAS

Blog spend makes ROAS look worse because value did not arrive, not because the target is wrong. If target ROAS is already missing, check destinations first. Exclude the folders, or turn expansion off only when most of the cost is off the product, then wait several days. Do not raise the target on the same day.

A small share of blog and policy cost needs exclusions, not a reset, including in the week before peak. If most of the cost is off the product, turn expansion off and accept a short wobble. Do not judge it the next morning, and do not pause Performance Max. You would also stop the product clicks.

Our published average is 18.3x ROAS, with floors of 13x and above in cycling, 12x and above in fishing and 14x and above in outdoor, and a best month of 35.3x. That is not a forecast for clicks sent to a returns page. Google Ads management is £1,500 a month. Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Performance Max sending traffic to the wrong pages?

Performance Max sending traffic to the wrong pages is usually Final URL expansion, which is on by default. Google can replace the URL in your asset group with another page on the same domain, including a blog, a size guide, a delivery page or a policy. A page feed does not stop that while expansion stays on. The feed limits traffic to itself and the asset group URLs only when expansion is off.

How do I turn off final URL expansion in Google Ads?

Open the Performance Max campaign, select the gear icon, then Campaign settings and Asset optimisation. Under Text, clear the Final URL checkbox and save. Leave text customisation on if the headlines are fine. If product pages are converting and only a few folders are the waste, add URL exclusions instead of turning expansion off.

Where do I see blog spend in the PMax landing page report?

The standard Landing pages report covers Search, Display, Video and Shopping, so it can miss Performance Max. Open Search terms in the Campaigns menu, choose Search terms and landing pages for Performance Max, and sort by cost. For URLs expansion swapped in, open the Expanded final URL assets tab. Look for blog, size guide, delivery, returns and policy URLs with spend and no conversion value.

Should I turn Final URL expansion off before Black Friday?

Not by default. If the waste is a small share of cost, add URL exclusion rules for those folders and leave expansion on, so product URLs you did not list can still serve. Turn it off when most of the cost lands off the product. Do not raise target ROAS in the same change, and read the URLs again after several days rather than the next morning.
