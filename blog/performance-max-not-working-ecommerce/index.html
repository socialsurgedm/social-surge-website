Why Your Performance Max Campaign Is Not Working (And How to Fix It)

Performance Max not delivering results for your e-commerce store? Common PMAX problems and fixes explained by a UK PPC agency with 18.3x average ROAS.

JBy Jack Goldsmith · Published 6 August 2026 · 7 min read

Why is my Performance Max campaign not working?

The most common causes are a target ROAS set too high for the conversion volume in the account (which prevents the campaign exiting the learning phase), product feed errors that stop Shopping inventory from serving, or Google concentrating budget on Display and YouTube instead of the Shopping placements that actually convert. Most underperforming PMAX campaigns can be improved by resetting the target ROAS, auditing the feed in Merchant Center, and adding proper audience signals to the asset groups.

Performance Max underperforms for e-commerce stores for the same handful of reasons, and they tend to compound each other. In the accounts we manage across cycling, fishing and outdoor retail, a PMAX campaign that's struggling is almost always suffering from at least two of three problems: signal starvation, a feed quality issue, or a target ROAS the campaign has no realistic path to hitting. This guide works through each one.

The Learning Phase Is Longer Than Most People Expect

Google officially states that the PMAX learning phase lasts around six weeks. In practice, for e-commerce stores generating fewer than 30-50 conversions per month, it can take considerably longer. The learning phase is not cosmetic: the algorithm is actively testing placements, audiences and asset combinations to find what converts. If you change the target ROAS, add or remove products, or significantly shift budget during this window, the clock resets.

The practical implication is that judging a PMAX campaign in its first four weeks is rarely fair. What you are looking for in weeks one to four is conversion volume and conversion quality, not ROAS. If conversions are arriving at roughly the volume you expected, the campaign is learning. If impressions are high but conversions are near zero, something structural is wrong.

Confirm conversion tracking before anything else

Before diagnosing any PMAX performance problem, confirm your conversion tracking is firing correctly. A campaign recording zero conversions when your GA4 shows completed purchases is a tracking problem, not a PMAX problem. Cross-reference Google Ads conversion actions against GA4 purchase events and confirm conversion value is passing through. It sounds basic, but broken or duplicated conversion tracking is consistently one of the first things we find in new accounts we take on.

Your Target ROAS Is Throttling the Campaign

The most common PMAX issue we encounter is a target ROAS set higher than the account can currently support. Target ROAS works as a floor the algorithm tries to stay above: if it cannot find enough conversions that clear that threshold, it simply stops bidding aggressively. The campaign appears active (impressions may even look healthy) but spend stalls and conversions dry up.

As a starting point, we typically set target ROAS at 200-300% for new PMAX campaigns in UK e-commerce accounts, then raise it incrementally in 10-20% steps every two weeks once volume is stable. Setting it at 800% on day one because that is what you achieved in a mature Standard Shopping campaign is one of the most reliable ways to ensure PMAX never finds its footing.

If your campaign has been live for eight or more weeks and is still spending well below budget, try reducing the target ROAS by 20-30% and holding it there for four weeks. In the accounts we manage, this single change has restored scale in campaigns that looked like write-offs. Give the algorithm room to work, then tighten the target once volume is flowing.

Feed Quality Is Usually the Underlying Problem

Performance Max relies on your Google Merchant Center product feed for its Shopping inventory. If the feed has widespread errors, disapproved products, missing GTINs or vague product titles, the algorithm has less high-quality Shopping inventory to bid on and defaults to non-shopping placements (Display, YouTube, Discovery) where it has more flexibility and fewer constraints.

In Merchant Center, go to Products then Diagnostics and review disapproval rates and item-level issues. Common feed problems that consistently hurt PMAX performance include:

  • Missing or incorrect GTINs on branded products, which reduces Shopping eligibility and match quality
  • Generic product titles that omit brand, product type and key attributes (size, colour, material), making them harder for Google to match to relevant searches
  • Price mismatches between the feed and the live product page, triggering disapprovals and pulling those products out of Shopping entirely
  • Missing sale prices when products are discounted, which loses the promoted price badge in Shopping results and reduces click-through rate

Google's product data quality guidelines are the authoritative reference for feed standards. For outdoor and sporting goods retailers in particular, adding product-specific attributes (activity type, weight range, size range, material) significantly improves Shopping match quality and, in turn, PMAX efficiency across the account.

Asset Groups and Audience Signals: How to Steer the Algorithm

Most store owners build one asset group, upload a logo and a few generic headlines, and assume PMAX handles the rest. It does handle the rest, but without direction, it defaults to broad audiences and lower-intent placements where signals are thinner.

Asset groups are how you steer Google's algorithm toward the right customers. Upload your best existing customer list as a Customer Match audience signal. If your list is too small for Customer Match (typically under 1,000 matched users), export a GA4 audience of past purchasers and use that instead. These signals do not restrict who the campaign targets; they show the algorithm what a valuable customer looks like so it can find more of them.

Beyond audience signals, separate high-margin product categories into their own asset groups rather than pooling everything together. Mixing premium cycling kit with entry-level accessories in one asset group means performance data gets blended, and the algorithm cannot allocate budget based on category-level ROAS differences. In the accounts we manage, splitting asset groups by margin tier or product category consistently produces better efficiency than a single catch-all group.

The asset quality itself also matters. Use specific, product-focused headlines rather than generic brand copy. Headlines like "Carbon Road Bikes from £1,200" outperform "Shop Our Range" because they carry search intent signals the algorithm can learn from.

Performance Max vs Standard Shopping: When to Switch Back

PMAX is not always the right structure for every e-commerce account. Consider pausing it and reverting to Standard Shopping when:

  • A Standard Shopping campaign running in parallel is outperforming PMAX on ROAS with comparable conversion volume, measured over at least eight weeks of concurrent data
  • Brand search is being heavily cannibalised by PMAX (visible in the Search Insights report as branded queries consuming a disproportionate share of budget), driving up blended CPA
  • The account generates fewer than 15-20 conversions per month, in which case PMAX does not have enough signal to learn efficiently regardless of how the campaign is configured

If you do pause PMAX, the natural successor for e-commerce is a well-structured Standard Shopping campaign with a separate Dynamic Search Ads campaign to capture long-tail queries. It is a less automated setup but gives significantly more control over where budget goes and which products it serves. Frequently Asked Questions

How long does Performance Max take to work for e-commerce?

Most accounts need 4-6 weeks and at least 30-50 conversions for PMAX to exit the learning phase. During this period, ROAS will typically be lower than your Shopping baseline. Expect 6-8 weeks before drawing firm conclusions about whether the campaign is working.

Should I run Performance Max and Standard Shopping campaigns at the same time?

It is possible, but PMAX takes priority over Standard Shopping for the same products. If you want to test how Standard Shopping performs independently, you need to either pause PMAX or use campaign-level product filters to separate inventory and prevent overlap.

How do I see where Performance Max is spending its budget?

Go to Campaign then Insights in Google Ads. The channel breakdown shows how budget is distributed across Shopping, Search, Display and YouTube. Full placement-level transparency is not available in PMAX, but the Insights tab shows the biggest budget concentrations and top-performing audience segments.

Can I stop Performance Max from showing on YouTube and Display?

Not directly. PMAX controls the channel mix through its own optimisation. You can influence it by improving feed quality and adding strong audience signals such as Customer Match lists, which tend to push budget toward Shopping inventory where ROAS is higher for most e-commerce brands.
