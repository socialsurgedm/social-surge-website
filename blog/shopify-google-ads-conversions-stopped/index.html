Shopify Google Ads Conversions Stopped Tracking: Why It Happened and How to Fix It

Shopify Google Ads conversions stopped tracking: the August 2026 additional scripts cutoff, how to confirm your tag died, and three paths to restore purchase data without double-counting.

JBy Jack Goldsmith · Published 8 November 2026 · 6 min read

ContentsWhat Changed on 26 August 2026How to Confirm Your Tag DiedHow to Fix It: Three PathsWhat Not to Do While the Tag Is BrokenHow Long Does Smart Bidding Take to Recover?Frequently Asked Questions

If your Shopify store's Google Ads conversions stopped tracking around 26 August 2026, Shopify's removal of Additional scripts is almost certainly the cause. Any conversion tag or GTM container pasted under Settings > Checkout no longer fires on the new thank-you and order-status pages. The fix is to migrate purchase tracking to the Google & YouTube channel app or a custom pixel under Settings > Customer events, neither of which requires editing Shopify's checkout theme files.

By Jack Goldsmith, Founder & Performance Marketer, Social Surge · 8 November 2026

In the accounts we manage, this change arrived without error messages in Shopify admin, which is what makes it dangerous. Orders kept coming in; the Google Ads purchase conversion count quietly dropped to zero or close to zero. By the time most store owners noticed, Smart Bidding had been training on a week or more of missing purchase data, and tROAS targets were already drifting. The good news is the fix is straightforward once you know what broke.

What Changed on 26 August 2026

Shopify rebuilt its thank-you and order-status pages as part of a broader checkout upgrade. The old pages supported a field called Additional scripts, found under Settings > Checkout, where store owners could paste any third-party tracking code: Google Ads conversion tags, GTM container snippets, pixels, enhanced conversions code, or anything else. That field is no longer supported on the new pages, and Shopify applied the cutoff automatically on 26 August 2026 for stores that had not already migrated voluntarily.

The result is straightforward but easy to miss: any tag in that field stopped executing from that date. Shopify's checkout itself still processes orders normally. The issue is entirely on the reporting side: conversions are happening, but the tags that tell Google Ads about them no longer fire. This is documented in Shopify's Additional scripts upgrade guide. Shopify's replacement for post-purchase tracking is its Customer events system, which uses app pixels or custom pixels rather than raw script injection.

How to Confirm Your Tag Died

Three checks take less than five minutes.

  • Google Ads Goals. Go to Goals, then Conversions. If your purchase conversion action shows "No recent conversions" or the count dropped sharply around 26 August, the Additional scripts cutoff is the likely cause, not campaign performance.
  • Shopify Additional scripts field. Go to Settings > Checkout and scroll to Additional scripts. If you see a Google Ads gtag snippet, a GTM container script, or anything beginning with AW-, that code is no longer running.
  • Settings > Customer events. If there is no Google app pixel or working custom pixel subscribed to checkout events, purchases are not being forwarded to Google at all. An empty Customer events page alongside a dead Additional scripts field confirms the break.

If you use enhanced conversions, also check whether the enhanced conversions tag was part of the Additional scripts setup. Enhanced conversions rely on the same firing path, so if the base conversion tag is dead, enhanced conversions are too, as covered in more detail in our guide to Google Ads conversion tracking for e-commerce.

How to Fix It: Three Paths

Path 1: Google & YouTube Channel App (Recommended)

The fastest fix for most Shopify stores is the Google & YouTube channel app, available free in the Shopify App Store. Install it, connect your Google Ads account, and under conversion measurement confirm that the purchase event is mapped to your conversion action and set as the primary action used for bidding. If you need to preserve a specific existing conversion action (one that carries historical data Smart Bidding has learned from), enter the conversion ID and label from the old tag under Add custom conversion ID and label. Google's guide on setting up conversion tracking through the Shopify channel covers each step.

Once the app is active, remove or disable any leftover code in Additional scripts and any old custom pixel that fires the same purchase event. Running duplicate conversion actions inflates reported ROAS and corrupts the signal Smart Bidding trains on. The app also supports enhanced conversions natively, which is worth enabling at the same time.

Path 2: Custom Pixel Under Customer Events

If you need parameters or logic the Google & YouTube channel cannot handle, add a custom pixel under Settings > Customer events. The pixel listens for the checkout_completed event and sends the conversion using gtag with order value, currency, and transaction ID for deduplication. Keep the custom pixel lean: a full GTM container often fails in the Customer events sandbox because it cannot interact with the page DOM the way old Additional scripts could. For standard Google Ads purchase tracking, the Google & YouTube channel is simpler and better maintained.

Path 3: Server-Side with an Order Webhook

For stores with high traffic or significant Consent Mode v2 blockage on browser-side events, a server-side setup passes conversions directly from Shopify's order/paid webhook to the Google Ads API, deduplicated on order ID. This removes the dependency on the browser loading the thank-you page at all. It requires developer time and ongoing maintenance, but it is the most reliable option and the one least affected by ad blockers, slow connections, or future Shopify page changes. Stores that have already seen browser-side enhanced conversions underperforming are the best candidates for this path.

What Not to Do While the Tag Is Broken

The temptation when conversion count drops sharply is to adjust bids or pause campaigns. Both responses make the eventual recovery harder.

Do not change your target ROAS. Smart Bidding is already operating on incomplete data. Raising tROAS in response to a measured drop in conversions tells the algorithm to be more selective on a signal it cannot see properly. When the tag is fixed and purchase conversions return, the algorithm then has to reconcile an aggressive target with a sudden data spike. This typically causes underspending or erratic bidding for several weeks, extending the disruption well beyond the original tracking gap. We cover why tROAS changes on bad data cause this effect in our post on why target ROAS stops working.

Do not pause the campaigns. Pausing resets the learning phase. When you unpause, the campaign restarts as if it were new, which extends recovery time significantly rather than shortening it.

If the tag has been broken for more than a week, use GA4 purchase data as a cross-reference for real revenue during the gap. GA4 uses a separate implementation path, so it is typically unaffected by the Additional scripts cutoff. This is also useful for understanding why platform ROAS and actual revenue diverge, as explained in our post on why GA4 and Google Ads conversions do not match.

How Long Does Smart Bidding Take to Recover?

Once the tag is fixed and purchases are recording, the algorithm needs enough fresh conversions to recalibrate. As a rough guide, campaigns typically need 20 to 30 new conversions over 2 to 4 weeks before tROAS bidding stabilises. Performance Max may take slightly longer because it has more signals to reconcile across channels.

During that window, hold targets and budgets steady. Fluctuating ROAS in the first two weeks after fixing the tag reflects the algorithm relearning, not a structural problem with the account. If tROAS remains unstable after 4 to 6 weeks of clean conversion data, that is worth investigating, but the investigation should start with whether the target was realistic to begin with, not with further bid changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why did my Shopify Google Ads conversions stop tracking in August 2026?

Shopify removed support for Additional scripts on the thank-you and order-status pages on 26 August 2026. Any Google Ads conversion tag, GTM container, or pixel pasted under Settings > Checkout stopped running from that date. Shopify replaced Additional scripts with Customer events pixels, which live under Settings > Customer events.

How do I fix Google Ads conversion tracking on Shopify's new thank-you page?

The quickest path is the Google and YouTube channel app. Install it from the Shopify App Store, connect your Google Ads account, and map the purchase event to your conversion action. Enter the conversion ID and label from the old tag if you need to preserve historical data in an existing conversion action. Remove any leftover code or duplicate custom pixel to avoid double-counting.

Will Smart Bidding or Performance Max recover after I fix the conversion tag?

Yes, but allow 2 to 4 weeks for campaigns to stabilise. Smart Bidding learns from recent conversion data, so if the tag was broken for weeks, campaigns will have been optimising on incomplete signals. Hold tROAS targets and budgets steady during the reset window and wait for at least 20 to 30 fresh conversions before drawing conclusions about performance.

Should I pause campaigns or change tROAS while tracking is broken?

Neither. Pausing resets campaign learning entirely. Changing tROAS forces Smart Bidding to work from the wrong signal, then forces another recalibration when conversions return. The right sequence is to fix the tracking tag first, then hold all campaign settings steady for two to three weeks while the conversion history rebuilds.
