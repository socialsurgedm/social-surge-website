Why Cycling PPC Is Different

Run a cycling account like a generic store and you'll bleed budget. Here's what actually matters.

Seasonal Demand

Cycling demand swings hard: spring and summer peaks, event-driven spikes, winter slumps. We scale budgets into the curve and pivot to indoor training, workshop services and accessories when the road season dies, instead of pausing spend and losing momentum.

High-AOV Bikes vs Accessories Mix

A complete bike at £2,000+ and a multi-pack of tubes at £8 need completely different bid strategies, tROAS targets and campaign structures. We segment by price tier and margin so Smart Bidding optimises each properly, not an average of both.

Feeds With Thousands of SKUs

Cycling catalogues sprawl: sizes, colourways, model years, brand restrictions. We structure product feeds with custom labels for margin, price tier and seasonality, keeping campaigns manageable and bids intentional across every SKU.

Product Exclusion Strategy

Real result: for one UK cycling retailer we identified and excluded 304 zero-conversion products. ROAS rose 34% and average order value climbed 54.8%, the budget simply stopped subsidising products that never sell.

FAQ

Cycling PPC Questions

Do Google Ads work for bike shops?

Yes, cycling is one of the strongest e-commerce categories for Google Ads because buyers search with specific, high-intent queries ("carbon gravel bike 56cm", "Shimano 105 groupset"). Managed well, cycling accounts routinely exceed 13x ROAS; our best client month reached 35.3x.

How do you handle cycling's seasonality?

We map budgets and bid targets to the cycling demand curve: scaling into spring/summer peaks, defending margin through winter, and shifting focus to indoor training, workshop and accessory demand in the off-season rather than blindly pausing spend.

What is a product exclusion strategy?

Systematically removing products that consume budget without converting. For one UK cycling retailer we excluded 304 zero-conversion products, ROAS rose 34% and average order value rose 54.8%, because budget concentrated on products that actually sell.

Can you manage feeds with thousands of bike SKUs?

Yes, large catalogues are the norm in cycling and exactly what our feed processes are built for. Custom labels for margin, price tier and seasonality keep a £2,000 bike and an £8 inner tube on entirely separate bidding strategies.
